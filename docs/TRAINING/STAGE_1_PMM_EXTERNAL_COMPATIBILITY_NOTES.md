# Stage 1 PMM — External Compatibility Notes

**Status:** research support / not canon / not validation  
**Date:** 15 September 2026  
**Parent:** `STAGE_1_PSYCHOLOGICAL_MECHANISM_MAP.md`

## Purpose

These notes test whether the Stage 1 process families conflict with established adjacent research. Compatibility does not validate Selection Point terminology, the five-stage architecture, the proposed three-family compression, or causal claims in any individual episode.

## 1. Automaticity is not one unitary mechanism

Moors & De Houwer, **Automaticity: Componential, Causal, and Mechanistic Explanations**, *Annual Review of Psychology* 67 (2016), DOI `10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033550`.

Relevant implication: automaticity has separable features and causal explanations; goals, attention, consciousness, time and representational factors need not cohere into one all-or-none state.

**Compatibility:** supports rejecting a hidden universal `fusion mechanism`.

## 2. Habit / default responding

Wood & Rünger, **Psychology of Habit**, *Annual Review of Psychology* 67 (2016), DOI `10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417`.

Relevant implication: context-linked repetition can produce efficient default responding, while habits and goal-directed action can coexist.

**Compatibility:** supports CP-3 as a candidate contributor, not as a universal explanation.

## 3. Appraisal and emotional response

Yeo & Ong, **Associations between cognitive appraisals and emotions: A meta-analytic review**, *Psychological Bulletin* 150(12) (2024), PMID `39404856`, DOI `10.1037/bul0000452`.

Relevant implication: appraisal dimensions show substantial associations with emotional responses, with meaningful heterogeneity across relations.

Scherer, **The Emotion Process: Event Appraisal and Component Differentiation**, *Annual Review of Psychology* 70 (2019), PMID `30110576`, DOI `10.1146/annurev-psych-122216-011854`.

**Compatibility:** supports treating appraisal as one contributor to perceived situation/action readiness; does not establish appraisal as the sole cause of action.

## 4. Self/identity and behavior

Snippe, Peters & Kok, **The operationalization of self-identity in reasoned action models: a systematic review...**, *Health Psychology and Behavioral Medicine* 9(1) (2021), PMID `34104549`, DOI `10.1080/21642850.2020.1852086`.

Relevant implication: self-identity constructs are frequently operationalized in ways that overlap with attitudes and perceived norms; conceptual independence must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

**Compatibility:** supports keeping `self-image / role` episode-local and provisional rather than using identity as a catch-all cause.

## 5. Decentering / metacognitive distinction

Mediators of acceptance and mindfulness-based therapies for anxiety and depression: systematic review and meta-analysis, *Clinical Psychology Review* (2022), PMID `35483275`, DOI `10.1016/j.cpr.2022.102156`.

Relevant implication: decentering is studied as a plausible mediator in specific intervention contexts.

**Compatibility:** supports the plausibility of TO-2-style differentiation of thought/interpretation from fact, but does not validate SP or imply that mindfulness is the required training method.

## 6. Functional inference must remain provisional

Contreras et al., **A systematic review of the correspondence between descriptive assessment and functional analysis**, *Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis* 56(1) (2023), PMID `36409837`, DOI `10.1002/jaba.958`.

Relevant implication: in the reviewed applied behavior-analysis literature, descriptive assessment did not perfectly correspond with experimental functional analysis; exact correspondence was reported in about half of comparisons.

**Transfer limitation:** this literature concerns functional assessment of problem behavior, often in clinical/developmental contexts. It cannot be directly generalized to ordinary SP episodes.

**Compatibility:** nevertheless reinforces the epistemic distinction:

```text
plausible function inferred from an episode
≠ experimentally established behavioral function
```

## 7. Intention is not action

Implementation-intention and action-control literature documents an intention–behavior gap and distinguishes goal commitment from execution.

Example: Baker, Charnin & Gollwitzer, **Changing Physician Behavior With Implementation Intentions: Closing the Gap Between Intentions and Actions**, *Academic Medicine* (2016), PMID `27008360`.

**Compatibility:** supports RC-018's boundary `selected continuation ≠ realized continuation`; does not validate the entire Selection Point core cycle.

## Research verdict

No reviewed adjacent literature establishes the proposed SP Stage 1 map as a validated causal theory. The literature is most compatible with a **multi-process, context-sensitive architecture** in which appraisal/state, active self/goal organization and habit/default responding can each contribute, while the exact contribution in a particular episode remains an empirical question.
