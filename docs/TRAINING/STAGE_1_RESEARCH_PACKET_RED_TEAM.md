## 1. Review question

The Red Team does not ask whether the Research Packet is elegant or comprehensive. It asks:

> Does the packet preserve approved Stage 1 meaning while exposing enough uncertainty to prevent Gate C from inventing psychological mechanisms merely by regrouping canon?

## 2. Verdict

**PASS WITH REQUIRED CONSTRAINTS.**

The packet is sufficiently complete for Gate B and accounts for all SP-S1-P01–P13, the Stage 1→2 boundary, RC-018, practice/transition boundaries, examples, evidence status and major risks.

However, its candidate mechanism section must **not** be promoted directly into the Psychological Mechanism Map. The five proposed M1–M5 clusters contain different ontological categories:

- candidate processes that may contribute to automatic action;
- phenomenological descriptions;
- trainable operations;
- reality-feedback disciplines;
- learning/assessment safeguards.

Calling all of them “psychological mechanisms” would create a category error.

## 3. Primary Red Team attacks

### RT-01 — “Automatic assembly / fusion” risks becoming a tautological mechanism

**Target:** M1.

The evidence supports the approved description that fact, state, interpretation, self-image, task and impulse can be insufficiently distinguished. It does **not** establish a single entity or causal mechanism called “fusion.”

Possible failure:

```text
person does not distinguish links
→ label this “fusion”
→ fusion explains why links are not distinguished
```

That would explain the description with a renamed version of the same description.

**Required constraint for Gate C:** separate:

```text
phenomenon: links are experienced as one necessity
candidate contributing processes: open hypotheses
trainable operation: distinguish relevant links
```

Do not require a learner to believe in “fusion” as a hidden mechanism.

---

### RT-02 — “Operational task” is the highest self-sealing risk in Stage 1

**Target:** M2 / P04.

Operational task is useful because it replaces moral judgment with functional inquiry. But it can become unfalsifiable if every action is retrospectively assigned a psychologically satisfying hidden function.

Example failure:

```text
person delayed launch
→ “therefore he protected his self-image”

person launched
→ “therefore he protected another self-image”
```

A model that explains every continuation after the fact cannot lose against reality.

**Required constraint for Gate C:** operational task remains a candidate functional hypothesis. A valid reconstruction must allow:

- “unknown”;
- several competing functions;
- objective necessity;
- physiological need;
- skill/resource deficit;
- simple habit;
- evidence that weakens the original hypothesis.

A Stage 1 learner does not need to discover a hidden true motive for the capability to be present.

---

### RT-03 — M3 is primarily a trainable capability, not an explanatory mechanism

**Target:** M3 “participation recovery through realistic availability.”

M3 is very close to the approved capability itself. Naming it a mechanism risks circularity:

```text
Stage 1 capability = recover participation
mechanism = participation recovery
```

**Required constraint for Gate C:** distinguish “what tends to close participation” from “what the learner must become able to do.”

The Psychological Mechanism Map may therefore need two linked columns/maps rather than one flat list:

```text
interfering / organizing processes
↔
trainable operations that change participation
```

---

### RT-04 — M4 is better treated as an epistemic/reality-loop discipline

**Target:** M4 “action → next-position feedback.”

RC-018 supplies a cross-cutting architecture for selection, realization, consequence and feedback. That architecture is critical, but it is not itself evidence for a distinct psychological mechanism.

**Required constraint for Gate C:** preserve RC-018 as a verification layer around every candidate mechanism/operation rather than automatically making it one mechanism among others.

A candidate explanation survives only while actual realization and consequences can contradict it.

---

### RT-05 — M5 belongs primarily to learning governance

**Target:** M5 / P12–P13.

Repeatability, limited scaffold, anti-intellectualization and anti-dependence are essential. But they describe how capability should be learned and judged, not necessarily how the original automatic episode is generated.

**Required constraint for Gate C:** treat P12/P13 as cross-cutting learning-quality and false-mastery safeguards unless later analysis shows a distinct trainable process that warrants mechanism status.

---

### RT-06 — P09 should not become “the body mechanism”

P09 establishes that bodily state and signals participate in the contour and must be distinguished from their meaning and habitual response. It does not establish a unique body pathway specific to Stage 1.

**Required constraint:** body remains a cross-cutting channel/context. Any specific causal body claim remains empirical and context-dependent.

---

### RT-07 — P07/P10 can accidentally pull Stage 1 into later trajectory work

P07 divergence and P10 life-position matter because local action has consequences. But excessive aggregation can require the learner to analyze patterns across time, direction stability and system structure before the Stage 1 operation is available.

**Required constraint:** in Stage 1, trajectory framing should answer only:

> What did this realized continuation make more likely, costly, easy, difficult or factual next?

It should not require Stage 2 scenario detection or Stage 4 multi-timeframe trajectory evaluation.

---

### RT-08 — AI can manufacture a convincing reconstruction

The current operating system gives Chat substantial research/synthesis power. The same danger exists in training: AI can infer a state, self-image, operational task and available move so convincingly that the participant merely agrees.

**Required constraint:** future training artifacts must separate:

```text
system-held structure
from
participant-produced content
```

The instrument may ask, reflect and preserve alternatives. It must not count its own reconstruction as learner capability.

---

### RT-09 — “Actually available” remains the hardest observability problem

The architecture correctly distinguishes imaginable from executable. But no validated procedure yet establishes whether a different continuation was psychologically available in a past episode.

**Required constraint:** Gate C must not solve this by definition. Availability should be treated as a graded, evidence-sensitive judgment using facts such as:

- was the alternative noticed;
- was it known/understood;
- were required resources present;
- was external support required/available;
- was a first step executable;
- did the person ever realize a comparable move under similar conditions.

Even then, historical availability remains uncertain when not observed in real time.

---

### RT-10 — The proposed three-layer simplification is useful but prematurely directional

The packet ends with a three-layer candidate:

```text
what closes the episode
→ what reopens participation
→ what keeps learning reality-coupled
```

This is a strong **learning architecture hypothesis**, but not yet a Psychological Mechanism Map. It may bias Gate C toward preserving M1–M5 rather than reconsidering them from first principles.

**Required constraint:** Gate C should start from a classification contract, not from the proposed cluster count.

## 4. Required Gate C classification contract

Before naming final mechanisms, every candidate item should be classified across at least these dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Phenomenon | What is actually experienced/observed? |
| Candidate mechanism | What process might explain it? |
| Epistemic status | Approved description, derived relation, or empirical hypothesis? |
| Trainable operation | What new operation must become available to the learner? |
| Observability | What would count as evidence the operation occurred? |
| Competing explanation | What else could explain the same episode? |
| Falsifier / weakening condition | What would make this mechanism/grouping less plausible? |
| Stage boundary | Does this require abilities from Stage 2–4? |

This prevents a canonical parameter from becoming a “mechanism” simply because it has a name.

## 5. Red Team compression hypothesis

After attack, the evidence supports a **more conservative working decomposition** for Gate C exploration:

### A. Candidate processes that can close the field of participation

Not one asserted mechanism, but a family of possible contributors:

```text
state / attentional narrowing
perceived-model certainty
self-image / role constraint
local functional priority
habitual response availability
objective constraints / resource limits
```

Some are approved descriptive distinctions; their precise causal contribution remains empirical.

### B. Core Stage 1 trainable operation

```text
localize one episode
→ distinguish enough of its assembly to stop treating it as a single necessity
→ identify one realistically available participation point
→ realize one continuation / partial continuation / conscious non-action
```

This is closer to the approved capability than any claim about one hidden mechanism.

### C. Reality and learning safeguards

```text
selected ≠ realized
consequence ≠ used feedback
outcome ≠ proof of capability
prompted ≠ independent
one episode ≠ mastery
explanation ≠ participation
one episode ≠ repeating scenario
```

These are cross-cutting constraints, not necessarily psychological mechanisms.

**Status:** HYPOTHESIS for Gate C, not approved architecture.

## 6. What the Research Packet successfully protects

The packet passes Gate B review because it:

- accounts for every P01–P13;
- explicitly distinguishes approved meaning from mechanism hypotheses;
- integrates RC-018 without rewriting old observations;
- protects the one-episode Stage 1 / multi-episode Stage 2 boundary;
- keeps examples illustrative rather than evidentiary;
- recognizes operational-task causality as uncertain;
- keeps P11/P12 inside their approved phase boundaries;
- exposes the AI/scaffold false-mastery problem;
- does not introduce numeric mastery thresholds or a composite score.
