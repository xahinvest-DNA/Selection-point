# Stage 1 — Measurement Reactivity / Questionnaire-as-Scaffold Observation

**ID:** SP-TR-S1-MR-OBS-001  
**Date:** 17 September 2026  
**Status:** observed_owner_self_report + research_required  
**Scope:** Stage 1 training architecture and owner self-pilot interpretation

## 1. Owner observation

During repeated daily use of the SP questionnaire, the Owner reported a qualitative shift:

- first days: the questionnaire was experienced mainly as an evening recall/reporting task;
- by approximately day 4: during daytime actions, an additional sense appeared of participating/attending to the action partly so that it could later be remembered and entered into the questionnaire.

This is a self-report observation, not proof of a universal mechanism.

## 2. Architectural significance

The questionnaire can no longer be treated as necessarily neutral telemetry.

More precise rule:

> **Questionnaire completion is not evidence of Selection Capacity, but repeated measurement itself may alter attention, memory, self-monitoring and behavior.**

Therefore:

```text
questionnaire = data collection
may also become
questionnaire = scaffold / intervention / cue
```

These roles must remain distinguishable.

## 3. Candidate mechanisms — hypotheses only

### H1 — measurement reactivity
Knowing that behavior will later be reported may change the behavior, cognition or attention being measured.

### H2 — anticipatory / prospective monitoring
The later reporting intention may remain partially active during the day, increasing attention to events that may need to be recalled later.

### H3 — salience / encoding change
Actions relevant to questionnaire categories may become more salient at the time they occur and therefore better encoded in memory.

### H4 — discrepancy-triggered self-regulation
Repeated questions may make a gap between intended and actual behavior more available, sometimes changing the continuation before evening reporting.

### H5 — external scaffold becoming an internal cue
With repetition, the questionnaire may begin to function as a learned cue that triggers noticing during the day. Whether this persists after removing the questionnaire is OPEN.

## 4. Critical distinction

A participant may improve while the questionnaire is present for several different reasons:

```text
Selection Capacity increased
OR
questionnaire prompted monitoring
OR
social/reporting demand changed behavior
OR
memory for behavior improved
OR
some combination
```

Outcome improvement alone cannot distinguish these.

## 5. Risks

### R1 — living for the questionnaire
Participant may optimize what will be reportable rather than what matters in reality.

### R2 — false capability attribution
Prompted noticing can be mistaken for independently available noticing.

### R3 — dependence on scaffold
The effect may disappear when the questionnaire/reminder is removed.

### R4 — observer overload
If the questionnaire produces continuous self-monitoring, it could conflict with the project goal of efficient automaticity and selective rather than permanent conscious supervision.

### R5 — social desirability / reporting distortion
Knowing what will be asked can alter reporting, behavior, or both.

## 6. Research implications

Future pilot design should preserve at least:

- questionnaire exposure / prompt exposure;
- whether noticing occurred before or after a prompt;
- whether the participant reports thinking about future reporting during the action;
- whether changed behavior persists on reduced-prompt or no-prompt periods;
- whether monitoring feels selective or continuous;
- whether the questionnaire changes actual target-domain behavior versus only recall/reporting.

No withdrawal schedule or experimental protocol is approved here.

## 7. Gate D implication

Learning Units should consider the possibility that a repeated reporting instrument can itself become a transitional training scaffold:

```text
external question
→ anticipatory noticing
→ in-the-moment cue
→ participant-owned re-entry
→ possible reduced dependence on external prompt
```

This is a hypothesis to test, not an approved learning law.

## 8. Updated questionnaire boundary

Replace the overly simple rule:

```text
questionnaire = telemetry only
```

with:

```text
questionnaire completion ≠ SP proof
questionnaire data = telemetry
questionnaire exposure may itself be an intervention/scaffold
prompt/scaffold effects must remain visible
```

## 9. Open research question

> **Can repeated measurement train the very noticing operation that Selection Point needs, and if so, when does externally prompted monitoring become internally available selective re-entry rather than dependence on the instrument?**
