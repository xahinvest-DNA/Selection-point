## 1. Trigger

Owner clarified two linked requirements:

1. The long-term goal is not to escape automaticity but to arrive at **effective automaticity**: useful decisions/actions become sufficiently trained that they can be initiated with low deliberative load in relevant contexts.
2. Before designing Stage 1 Learning Units, the project must seriously investigate **how an existing automatic continuation is interrupted or displaced**, especially when intention is strong but actual behavior still reverts to the prior route.

## 2. Research questions

### RQ1 — Intention gap
Why can a strong intention fail to alter realized behavior when an established automatic route is cued?

### RQ2 — Interruption
What empirically supported classes of intervention can create an interruption window in an already learned continuation?

Candidate classes to examine, without assuming efficacy:
- cue/context change;
- environmental friction / stimulus control;
- implementation intentions / if-then plans;
- competing or substitute response;
- response inhibition / stopping;
- prompts / retrieval cues;
- awareness / present-moment detection;
- consequence/reward restructuring;
- support / accountability;
- deliberate waiting or conscious non-action.

### RQ3 — Persistence of old learning
Does successful interruption erase the old automatic route, suppress it, compete with it, or create context-dependent new learning?

### RQ4 — New automaticity
What conditions support a selected alternative becoming increasingly automatic, efficient and low-load?

### RQ5 — Transfer and relapse
How should training handle context specificity, stress/load, return to old cues, lapses and generalization to varied real-world conditions?

### RQ6 — SP fit
Which parts belong to Stage 1, which are later-stage requirements, and which are only designer-side research?

## 3. Critical distinctions

Research must preserve:

```text
intention ≠ realization
interruption ≠ extinction of old learning
non-performance ≠ new automaticity
new response once ≠ habit/skill
habit ≠ all automaticity
skill automaticity ≠ necessarily rigid habit
awareness ≠ interruption
interruption ≠ correct replacement
replacement ≠ transfer across contexts
lapse ≠ proof that training failed
```

## 4. Automaticity neutrality

No source may be used to reframe automaticity as pathology by default.

The target hypothesis is:

> Selection Point may function as a transition architecture from an existing automatic continuation to a more reality-aligned, increasingly automatic continuation.

This is a hypothesis, not approved canon.

## 5. Evidence discipline

Prioritize:
- systematic reviews/meta-analyses;
- controlled experiments;
- longitudinal studies;
- major theoretical/review papers with clear empirical grounding.

Distinguish:
- human evidence;
- animal/basic-learning evidence;
- clinical-domain evidence that may not transfer to general SP use;
- domain-specific evidence (e.g. alcohol, eating, smoking, motor skill, expert decision-making).

No single clinical treatment is to be universalized into SP.
