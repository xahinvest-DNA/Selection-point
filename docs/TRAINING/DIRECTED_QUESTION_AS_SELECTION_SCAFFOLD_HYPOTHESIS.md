# Deferred Research Hypothesis — Directed Question as Selection Scaffold

**ID:** SP-TR-HYP-002  
**Status:** parked / owner-approved research hypothesis  
**Date:** 20 September 2026  
**Scope:** preserve a research direction without modifying the approved Stage 1 architecture, Gate E Practice Protocol, Gate F measurement system, or Gate H decision space.

## 1. Trigger

The Owner raised a hypothesis after encountering a public social-media example built around self-directed questions rather than declarative affirmations.

The practical contrast was:

```text
declarative affirmation
"I am / I will ..."

vs

directed self-question
"What / how / when / why ...?"
```

The specific research interest is not whether questions "create reality." It is whether a question can alter what information becomes cognitively available, what is noticed or retrieved, and therefore which continuation becomes available for actual realization.

## 2. Core hypothesis

> **A well-constructed, reality-coupled question may function as an attentional / search / retrieval scaffold that changes the currently available field of continuations without prescribing the answer.**

Candidate mechanism:

```text
question
→ constrains / redirects search
→ changes accessibility of relevant information
→ may change noticing or retrieval
→ may expose an open edge
→ may alter selected continuation
→ may alter realized continuation
→ reality produces consequences
→ consequences may become feedback
```

This is a hypothesis, not an approved causal law.

## 3. Critical distinction: open question vs presuppositional question

Many apparently "open" motivational questions contain a built-in premise.

Example structure:

```text
"Why is X already true?"
```

The answer is grammatically open, but the premise `X is already true` is not.

This creates two different classes.

### A. Reality-coupled directed question

Examples:

- What is happening now that matters for the next continuation?
- What has already happened, and what is still open?
- What continuation is actually available under the current facts and constraints?
- What can I realize now so that reality can answer?
- What changed after the action?

These questions direct search while preserving the possibility that the answer is:

```text
no
unknown
not available
too late for this edge
wait
seek support
revise direction
do not act
```

### B. Positive presuppositional question

Examples in generic form:

- Why am I so successful?
- Why does everything work in my favor?
- How does it feel to already possess the desired outcome?

These may direct attention toward confirming material while suppressing contradiction.

For Selection Point, that is a major epistemic risk.

## 4. Evidence map

### 4.1 Positive self-statements can backfire in some conditions

Wood, Perunovic & Lee (2009) found that repeating a highly positive self-statement could make some participants with low self-esteem feel worse, while benefits for participants with high self-esteem were limited.

**Use for SP:** declarative positive statements are not assumed to be universally beneficial.

Reference:  
Wood JV, Perunovic WQE, Lee JW. *Positive self-statements: power for some, peril for others.* Psychological Science. 2009;20(7):860-866. PMID 19493324. DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02370.x.

### 4.2 Interrogative self-talk has an interesting original result but weak replication

Senay, Albarracín & Noguchi (2010) reported better task performance / motivation after interrogative rather than declarative self-talk in several experiments.

However, Van Raalte et al. (2018) ran six replication/extension studies and found no reliable advantage of interrogative over declarative self-talk; their meta-analysis across those studies also found no significant difference among conditions.

**Use for SP:** do not adopt the rule `question > statement` as established fact. The important variable may be context, content, generated reasons, relevance, or another feature rather than grammatical question form itself.

References:  
Senay I, Albarracín D, Noguchi K. *Motivating goal-directed behavior through introspective self-talk.* Psychological Science. 2010;21(4):499-504. PMID 20424090. DOI 10.1177/0956797610364751.  
Van Raalte JL et al. *I Will Use Declarative Self-Talk ... or Will I? Replication, Extension, and Meta-Analyses.* The Sport Psychologist. 2018;32(1):16-25. DOI 10.1123/tsp.2016-0088.

### 4.3 Asking questions can itself change later behavior

An updated systematic review/meta-analysis of question-behavior effects found a small overall effect of measurement on later health-related behavior (43 studies; n=104,388; SMD approximately 0.06), with substantial heterogeneity and evidence of publication bias.

**Use for SP:** measurement/question exposure may be an intervention, but the effect is small and inconsistent at the literature level.

Reference:  
Rodrigues AM et al. *Asking questions changes health-related behavior: an updated systematic review and meta-analysis.* Journal of Clinical Epidemiology. 2020.

### 4.4 Presuppositions in questions can bias later reports

Classic work by Loftus showed that questions containing true or false presuppositions could alter later eyewitness reports.

**Use for SP:** a question is not epistemically neutral merely because it has a question mark. Its embedded premise may change search, memory, interpretation or report.

Reference:  
Loftus EF. *Leading questions and the eyewitness report.* Cognitive Psychology. 1975;7(4):560-572. DOI 10.1016/0010-0285(75)90023-7.

### 4.5 Desired-future imagery is not sufficient

Research reviewed by Oettingen distinguishes pleasant positive fantasy from mental contrasting that reconnects the desired future to present reality and obstacles.

**Use for SP:** future-directed cognition should remain reality-coupled rather than substituting an imagined desired state for the factual current position.

Reference:  
Oettingen G, Reininger KM. *The power of prospection: mental contrasting and behavior change.* Social and Personality Psychology Compass. 2016;10(11):591-604. DOI 10.1111/spc3.12271.

## 5. Connection to the existing SP measurement-reactivity finding

This hypothesis directly extends `SP-TR-S1-MR-OBS-001`.

Existing observation:

```text
evening questionnaire
→ future reporting becomes salient during the day
→ attention / participation may change before the questionnaire is completed
```

Candidate interpretation:

```text
external repeated question
→ learned anticipatory retrieval cue
→ question becomes internally available during a live event
→ possible NOTICE / OPEN support
```

This remains compatible with several alternative explanations:

- accountability / demand characteristics;
- social desirability;
- increased prospective monitoring;
- improved memory encoding;
- simple reminder effect;
- genuine selective re-entry;
- a combination of these.

No one mechanism is approved.

## 6. Candidate SP design principle

A useful SP question should:

1. direct attention to a relevant edge;
2. reduce unnecessary search rather than demand total self-monitoring;
3. not insert a desired fact as if already true;
4. preserve `unknown`, `no`, `not available`, and direction revision as valid answers;
5. preserve current facts, constraints and safety;
6. end in a possible reality-coupled continuation, not only insight;
7. allow consequences to contradict the participant's expectation.

Compact boundary:

> **The question may guide search, but reality must retain the right to reject its premise, its expected answer, and the selected continuation.**

## 7. Candidate experiment — not yet authorized

A later post-protocol study could compare matched interventions in one narrow training lane:

```text
A — declarative:
"I will realize the prepared continuation."

B — positive presuppositional:
"Why is it becoming easy for me to realize the prepared continuation?"

C — reality-coupled SP question:
"When the old continuation appears, what is still open right now?"
```

Possible comparison condition:

```text
D — neutral reminder / no directed question
```

Required observables should remain compatible with Gate F:

```text
NOTICE timing
OPEN / open-edge status
support provenance
question salience
selected continuation
realized continuation
selected → realized relation
immediate consequence
later consequence where relevant
UPDATE
```

The experiment should distinguish:

```text
question changed report
≠
question changed noticing
≠
question changed selection
≠
question changed realization
```

## 8. Falsification signals

The hypothesis weakens if:

- directed questions do not improve live NOTICE/OPEN/REALIZE traces relative to matched reminders or declarative forms;
- effects appear only in retrospective reporting, not in behavior;
- presuppositional forms improve positive self-description while worsening factual calibration;
- question exposure increases continuous self-monitoring or burden;
- the effect disappears immediately when prompts are removed and no participant-initiated retrieval is observed;
- questions produce rigid adherence to a prepared continuation despite new facts;
- a simpler cue/reminder performs equally well with less cognitive burden.

## 9. Anti-claims

This hypothesis does **not** authorize the claims that:

- questions create external reality;
- the brain must answer any question it receives;
- interrogative self-talk is universally superior to declarative self-talk;
- positive presuppositional questions are inherently beneficial;
- a changed feeling or report proves changed behavior;
- questionnaire reactivity proves Stage 1 capability;
- frequent conscious questioning is the desired end state.
