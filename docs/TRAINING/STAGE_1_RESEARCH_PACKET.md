## Epistemic legend

- **APPROVED** — explicitly approved Selection Point meaning.
- **DERIVED** — conservative synthesis directly implied by approved material.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — candidate explanation or grouping requiring review/evidence.
- **OPEN** — unresolved question.
- **CONTRADICTION** — meaningful tension that cannot safely be removed by synthesis.
- **ILLUSTRATION** — example useful for explanation but not empirical proof.
- **OBSERVED** — externally or directly recorded fact where such evidence exists.
- **SELF-REPORT** — participant report about internal state, intention or experience.
- **INTERPRETATION** — explanatory reading of facts/self-report; not equivalent to observed fact.

# A. Executive research summary

## A1. What Stage 1 is trying to make practically possible

**APPROVED** — Stage 1 capability is:

> **Человек учится превращать один собственный автоматический эпизод из переживаемой неизбежности в различимый процесс, находить первый реально доступный участок своего участия и фактически менять хотя бы одно дальнейшее продолжение.**

Stage 1 therefore does not merely teach psychological vocabulary. Its practical movement is:

```text
переживаемая неизбежность
→ конкретный эпизод
→ различимые звенья
→ реалистично доступный участок участия
→ выбранное продолжение
→ фактически реализованное продолжение
→ последствия
→ данные / feedback для следующего цикла
```

**DERIVED** — the Stage 1 architecture contains several different kinds of material that must not be mechanically converted into separate lessons or mechanisms:

1. P01–P06 primarily describe the assembly and experienced inevitability of an automatic episode;
2. P07 describes a trajectory-level pain/signal that may emerge from repeated divergence;
3. P08 specifies the next practical operation: reconstruction of one episode;
4. P09 and P10 expose the same process at body and life/trajectory scales;
5. P11 defines functional requirements for future practice, not a finished exercise;
6. P12 defines a qualitative readiness profile, not numeric mastery criteria;
7. P13 defines a stage-specific learning trap: explanation can replace participation.

## A2. What Stage 1 explicitly does not claim

**APPROVED** — Stage 1 does not require or prove:

- disappearance of automatic reactions;
- permanent self-awareness;
- control over first thoughts, emotions, impulses or bodily reactions;
- prevention of every first automatic move;
- total control of outcomes;
- that a person creates all external events or difficulties;
- that any single hidden motive explains behavior;
- that bodily signals are infallible or diagnostic;
- that retrospective alternatives were necessarily available in the original state;
- that understanding or correct SP vocabulary equals capability;
- that one successful episode equals mastery;
- that prompted/scaffolded performance equals independent capability;
- that Stage 1 already requires recognition of a repeating scenario across episodes;
- numeric transition thresholds, a score or validated assessment procedure.

**EVIDENCE BOUNDARY** — `SP-S1-P01–P13` are internally approved but remain `external-review-pending` and `pilot-observability-pending`. Internal coherence is not external scientific, clinical or universal validation.

## A3. Central research question for Gate C

**OPEN — must be decided in Chat before Gate C approval:**

> **What is the smallest coherent set of trainable psychological operations/processes needed to produce the approved Stage 1 capability, without treating the 13 canonical parameters as 13 mechanisms, turning functional hypotheses into causal facts, or allowing explanation to substitute for realized participation?**

A second-order question is equally important:

> **Which Stage 1 parameters describe mechanisms to be trained, and which instead describe context, scale, observable consequence, practice requirements, readiness criteria or anti-traps?**

# B. Canon inventory — SP-S1-P01–SP-S1-P13

## SP-S1-P01 — state: life inside automatic reaction

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
A Stage 1 episode is experienced as a largely fused event in which external conditions, state, attention, interpretation, self-image, operational task, impulse and action are insufficiently distinguished. Automaticity is not equivalent to weak will or passivity; a person may be highly active, controlling or forceful while still operating automatically.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Provides the primary object of study: one concrete episode that initially feels like “this simply happened / I just had to do it.”

**Relation to capability**  
The capability begins by turning this fused event into a distinguishable process.

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/02_STAGE_1_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/01_STAGE_1_STATE.md`; HCM-01–HCM-04.

**Important boundaries**
- automaticity ≠ laziness or lack of intelligence;
- unpleasant emotion is not required;
- a strong/decisive action may still be automatic;
- state changes accessibility but does not by itself establish the cause of behavior.

**OPEN ambiguity for Gate C**  
Whether “fusion” should be treated as one trainable mechanism or as the observable result of several interacting processes (state-dependent attention, appraisal, self-schema activation, habitual response availability).

---

## SP-S1-P02 — perceived model vs reality

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
The person acts relative to a perceived model of the situation and often experiences that model as the situation itself. Fact, state, interpretation, causal story, prognosis and action implication can collapse into one experienced reality.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Creates the first epistemic separation required for reconstruction:

```text
fact
≠ state
≠ interpretation
≠ causal explanation
≠ prediction
```

**Relation to capability**  
An episode cannot become distinguishable if interpretation remains indistinguishable from external fact.

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/02_STAGE_1_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/02_STAGE_1_REALITY_PERCEPTION.md`; HCM-01/HCM-02.

**Important boundaries**
- subjective model does not mean objective reality is unknowable;
- the first interpretation may be correct;
- a feeling is a fact of internal experience but is not sufficient proof of the external interpretation;
- the purpose is not endless doubt but testable distinction.

**OPEN ambiguity for Gate C**  
Should fact/model distinction be a standalone learning mechanism, or the epistemic operation embedded in every other Stage 1 reconstruction?

---

## SP-S1-P03 — activated self-image vs whole self

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
A contextually activated self-image/role can be experienced as the whole self. A local action or failure can become a global identity statement, and the self-image can narrow which actions feel psychologically permissible.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Prevents reconstruction from ending at “I am this kind of person” and makes role/self-image one possible element of the episode rather than its final explanation.

**Relation to capability**  
Restores room between an activated identity-position and the next continuation.

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/02_STAGE_1_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/03_STAGE_1_SELF_PERCEPTION.md`; HCM-04.

**Important boundaries**
- self-image may contain accurate information and is not simply false;
- the method does not require a metaphysical “true self” claim;
- seeing a self-image does not prove that it caused the action;
- positive identities can constrain action as much as negative ones.

**OPEN ambiguity for Gate C**  
Whether self-image activation is a distinct mechanism or a content channel through which the broader automatic assembly acquires force.

---

## SP-S1-P04 — dominant operational task

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
Behavior may be organized by a local operational task that currently outranks a broader declared direction. Examples include rapidly reducing state tension, restoring control, protecting a self-image, avoiding evaluation or preserving a role. The broader goal may remain sincere while losing local decision priority.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Shifts analysis from moral judgment (“why am I weak?”) toward a functional question: what did this action appear to solve or obtain now?

**Relation to capability**  
Makes the immediate functional payoff of habitual action visible and creates the possibility of another continuation that addresses reality rather than merely suppressing the same task.

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/02_STAGE_1_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/04_STAGE_1_DECISION_DRIVERS.md`; HCM-03/HCM-04.

**Important boundaries**
- operational task is a **hypothesis**, not directly observed hidden truth;
- no universal motive (fear, pleasure, control, trauma, self-image protection) is allowed;
- the action may solve a real short-term problem;
- rational short-term tradeoffs and objective constraints remain competing explanations.

**OPEN ambiguity for Gate C**  
Whether training should explicitly teach “operational task” as a concept or teach a more neutral functional-analysis question to reduce suggestion and post-hoc confabulation.

---

## SP-S1-P05 — actually available point of participation

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
The relevant choice point is the first distinguishable and practically executable place where further continuation can still change. It may appear before an action, during it, after it, while limiting consequences, repairing damage, seeking help or changing the next cycle.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Turns reconstruction into participation and prevents all-or-nothing evaluation of “I already failed.”

**Relation to capability**  
Directly instantiates the capability phrase “находить первый реально доступный участок своего участия.”

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/02_STAGE_1_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/05_STAGE_1_AVAILABLE_POINT_OF_CHOICE.md`; HCM-07; `SP-RISK-019`, `SP-RISK-020`, `SP-RISK-023`.

**Important boundaries**

```text
objectively possible
≠ noticed
≠ psychologically available
≠ actually executable
```

- late detection remains valid;
- imagined hindsight alternatives are not evidence they were available in the original state;
- real restrictions must not be called illusions;
- asking for support may itself be the available move.

**OPEN ambiguity for Gate C**  
What minimal operation lets a learner estimate availability without either excusing every non-action or retroactively moralizing it?

---

## SP-S1-P06 — illusion of inevitable continuation

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
A current assembly can be experienced as objective necessity: the current model feels like reality, the activated role like the whole self, the operational task like a demand of the situation, and the habitual means like the only continuation.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Names the subjective closure that reconstruction attempts to reopen.

**Relation to capability**  
Stage 1 changes “there was no continuation” into a more precise question: where, if anywhere, did another continuation become actually available?

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/02_STAGE_1_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/06_STAGE_1_MAIN_ILLUSION.md`; P01–P05.

**Important boundaries**
- experienced inevitability can coexist with forcefulness and confidence;
- some situations genuinely have very narrow or single safe options;
- the existence of a theoretically imaginable option does not prove freedom in the episode.

**DERIVED relation**  
P06 appears more like the emergent phenomenology of P01–P05 than an independent causal mechanism.

**OPEN ambiguity for Gate C**  
Whether P06 should be a mechanism cluster, an experiential marker, or a synthesis outcome used to teach the learner what closure feels like from inside.

---

## SP-S1-P07 — pain of divergence between intention and trajectory

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
A central Stage 1 pain may emerge when a meaningful direction remains important while repeated local continuations create a different trajectory. The pain is not simple deviation from any stated goal; goals can rationally change and external conditions matter.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Makes the cost of repeated locally functional actions visible across time without turning pain or shame into the training method.

**Relation to capability**  
Provides one source of relevance for reconstructing an episode and linking a local continuation to a next position.

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/02_STAGE_1_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/07_STAGE_1_MAIN_PAIN.md`; HCM-05/HCM-08; `SP-RISK-021`.

**Important boundaries**
- strong pain is not required;
- pain does not prove the declared direction is genuinely chosen;
- distress must not be deliberately amplified for motivation;
- external barriers can account for divergence.

**DERIVED relation**  
P07 is primarily a consequence/signal at trajectory scale rather than a candidate psychological mechanism that must be trained directly.

**OPEN ambiguity for Gate C**  
How much Stage 1 learning needs explicit direction/trajectory framing versus staying with a single episode until later stages.

---

## SP-S1-P08 — reconstruction of one automatic episode

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
The next skill is to reconstruct one concrete automatic episode as a functional sequence rather than a personality verdict. A useful reconstruction may distinguish conditions, state, perceived situation, activated self-image, operational task, habitual continuation, immediate result, deferred price and next position.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Provides the main learning operation through which the automatic event becomes inspectable.

**Relation to capability**  
Directly instantiates “превращать один собственный автоматический эпизод ... в различимый процесс.”

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/02_STAGE_1_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/08_STAGE_1_NEXT_SKILL.md`; P01–P07.

**Important boundaries**
- reconstruction is not proof of causality;
- operational task and internal causes remain hypotheses;
- one episode is the unit; recurring scenario detection belongs to Stage 2;
- reconstruction that ends at understanding is incomplete for Stage 1 training.

**DERIVED relation**  
P08 may be better treated as the integrative learning operation that coordinates several mechanisms rather than as another mechanism itself.

---

## SP-S1-P09 — body across the automatic contour

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
The body participates across the episode: baseline state, reaction, felt need, short-term effect of a habitual action, delayed bodily consequence and next-cycle state can all matter. A sensation, its interpreted meaning, a real need and the habitual means used to meet it are not the same thing.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Adds an embodied data channel and exposes how a short-term bodily payoff can make a continuation locally compelling.

**Relation to capability**  
Improves reconstruction and may reveal additional participation points without making body regulation equivalent to choice.

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/02_STAGE_1_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/09_STAGE_1_BODY_MANIFESTATIONS.md`; HCM-02; `SP-RISK-013`.

**Important boundaries**
- no universal bodily marker of automaticity or choice;
- bodily signal ≠ diagnosis;
- obeying every impulse and suppressing every impulse are both rejected;
- physiological need may be real;
- body-state relationships may be reciprocal and multi-causal.

**DERIVED relation**  
P09 is primarily a cross-cutting observation channel/context, not necessarily a standalone Stage 1 mechanism.

---

## SP-S1-P10 — current life as accumulated trajectory position

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
A current life position can include objective circumstances, actions of other people, previous own actions/inaction and accumulated consequences. A difficulty becomes an entry point for inquiry into how the position formed and what continuation is being created now; it is not proof of personal blame or total authorship.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Connects one episode to material consequences and prevents the method from becoming purely intrapsychic.

**Relation to capability**  
Shows why a changed continuation matters: realized actions alter the next position from which later choices occur.

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/02_STAGE_1_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/10_STAGE_1_LIFE_MANIFESTATIONS.md`; HCM-05/HCM-06/HCM-08; `SP-RISK-001`, `SP-RISK-022`.

**Important boundaries**
- participation in trajectory ≠ total causation or control;
- illness, violence, crisis, loss and actions of others keep independent causal status;
- a current difficulty does not determine stage;
- sequence alone does not prove causality.

**RC-018 clarification**  
Factual trajectory is built from realized continuations and their consequences, not merely from sincere internal choices.

**DERIVED relation**  
P10 primarily supplies scale and reality coupling; it should not automatically become a separate mechanism lesson.

## SP-S1-P11 — functional profile of corresponding practices

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
Future Stage 1 practices must move from global self-judgment to localization of one episode, reconstruction of distinguishable links, embodied contour, action-to-next-position relation and one actually available realized participation. Practice cannot end at explanation.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Defines what any future practice must accomplish.

**Relation to capability**  
Operationalizes the minimum path from distinction to participation.

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/03_STAGE_1_P11_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/11_STAGE_1_CORRESPONDING_PRACTICES.md`; `PRACTICE_PARAMETER_BOUNDARY.md`.

**Important boundary**  
P11 is **not** a finished exercise architecture. It does not approve names, step-by-step instructions, duration, frequency, worksheets, delivery format, interface or mastery criteria. Those belong to later phases.

**RC-018 clarification**  
A selected alternative is not sufficient; actual realization, partial realization, conscious non-action or another factual continuation must be distinguished from internal choice.

**DERIVED relation**  
P11 is a design constraint for training, not an independent psychological mechanism.

## SP-S1-P12 — qualitative transition/readiness profile

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
Readiness for the next stage appears when the person can repeatedly, with limited structural support, reconstruct concrete episodes, preserve fact/hypothesis distinctions, identify realistically available participation, produce observable influence in at least some real episodes, and hold several episodes as separate comparable units.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Defines what later measurement should attempt to observe and protects the Stage 1/Stage 2 boundary.

**Relation to capability**  
Requires the Stage 1 operation to become repeatably available rather than occur once by chance or because a facilitator performed it.

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/04_STAGE_1_P12_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/12_STAGE_1_TRANSITION_CRITERIA.md`; `TRANSITION_CRITERIA_BOUNDARY.md`; `SP-RISK-024`, `SP-RISK-025`.

**Important boundaries**
- no numeric threshold, duration, pass rate, score or test is approved;
- support may hold structure but may not create content/interpretation/action for the person;
- better outcomes alone do not prove capability;
- total autonomy is not required;
- Stage 2 pattern recognition is not yet required.

**DERIVED relation**  
P12 is a consolidation/observability constraint, not a primary automaticity mechanism.

## SP-S1-P13 — trap of explained automatism

**APPROVED canonical meaning**  
The Stage 1 capability can become a new automatic contour when explanation, insight, correct terminology, self-blame, control or observer-status provides immediate relief and substitutes for participation. A person may understand the episode better while the trajectory remains materially unchanged.

**Practical function inside Stage 1**  
Prevents the learning system from rewarding verbal sophistication or AI-assisted interpretation as if it were capability.

**Relation to capability**  
Protects the final requirement: the process must reach realized participation and usable reality feedback.

**Primary sources**  
`CANONICAL/05_STAGE_1_P13_CANONICAL.md`; `FIVE_STAGES_THEORY/13_STAGE_1_STAGE_TRAP.md`; Stage 1 completion checkpoint; `SP-RISK-008`, `SP-RISK-010`, `SP-RISK-016`, `SP-RISK-025`, `SP-RISK-026`, `SP-RISK-027`.

**Approved short formula**

> **Я понял, как это происходит, — и снова заменил выбор пониманием.**

**Approved observer-superiority risk**

> **Я вижу автоматизм других, но не превращаю своё видение в участие в собственной жизни.**

**DERIVED relation**  
P13 is primarily a meta-learning/anti-substitution mechanism and quality constraint, not the mechanism that initially generates the automatic episode.

# C. Cross-cutting boundaries affecting Stage 1

## C1. Fact vs perception/model

**APPROVED**

```text
external fact
≠ internal state
≠ perceived model
≠ interpretation
≠ causal hypothesis
```

Training must not replace one unquestioned interpretation with another SP interpretation.

## C2. Activated self-image vs whole self

**APPROVED**

```text
activated role / self-image
≠ whole person
≠ proven cause of the action
```

The useful question is how a self-image affected what felt permissible or necessary in this episode, not what the person's “real identity” is.

## C3. Actually available participation point

**APPROVED**

```text
imaginable option
≠ noticed option
≠ psychologically available option
≠ executable option
```

Late participation can be valid. Loss of choice at one point does not imply loss of participation in every later continuation.

## C4. Explanation vs participation

**APPROVED**

```text
accurate explanation
≠ Stage 1 capability
```

Reconstruction is instrumental: it must return to an available continuation, consequence or next-cycle condition. P13 makes this boundary central.

## C5. Selected vs realized continuation — RC-018

**APPROVED later cross-cutting authority**

```text
selected continuation
≠ realized continuation
```

Internal choice is a real event, but it is not evidence that the action occurred. A selected-realized gap is data, not automatic proof of sabotage or weak will.

## C6. Realized continuation vs consequence

**APPROVED**  
Execution does not guarantee the desired outcome or a clean causal answer. Unexpected events, other actors and noisy/delayed outcomes remain possible.

## C7. Consequence vs used feedback

**APPROVED**

```text
consequence
≠ automatically used feedback
```

A consequence becomes used feedback only when it is noticed/interpreted and materially enters correction or preservation of the next-cycle model/continuation.

## C8. Conscious non-action and partial realization

**APPROVED**
- deliberate non-action may be a realized continuation when non-intervention is what was actually selected and sustained;
- partial execution yields a partial test, not an automatic success/failure verdict.

## C9. Observed fact vs self-report vs inference vs interpretation

**APPROVED / Product Lab compatible boundary**  
Stage 1 frequently works with internal phenomena that cannot be externally observed directly. Therefore:

- behavior/time/context may be `OBSERVED` or recorded;
- internal state/intention may be `SELF-REPORT`;
- operational task, causal link and hidden function are generally `INTERPRETATION/HYPOTHESIS`;
- absence of evidence should remain `unknown/not_observed` rather than be filled by narrative coherence.

# D. Source examples and cases

Foundation examples are **ILLUSTRATIONS**, not participant-level empirical validation. The example library explicitly states that examples explain approved architecture but do not prove universality.

## D1. SP-EX-REL-001 — short reply from another person

**Factual/observed layer:** another person replied briefly.  
**Possible participant interpretations:** “busy”, “angry”, “doesn't respect me”, “rejects me”, “insufficient data”.  
**Project interpretation:** one external event can support several perceived situations and therefore different action fields.  
**Supports:** fact/model distinction (P02).  
**Cannot support:** that the negative interpretation is wrong, that any one alternative is correct, or that the model caused a later action.

## D2. SP-EX-PROJECT-001 — endless preparation / large-potential self-image

**Factual layer in the illustration:** finishing/showing/selling is deferred while preparation/complexity continues.  
**Participant interpretation:** “it's really not time yet.”  
**Project interpretation:** one candidate functional reading is that preserving a high-potential self-image makes evaluation threatening and preparation locally functional.  
**Supports:** how P03/P04/P06 can be illustrated together.  
**Cannot support:** that careful preparation generally equals identity protection or that “fear of evaluation” is the true cause in a real person without evidence.

## D3. SP-EX-TRADE-001 — loss followed by urge to regain control

**Factual layer in the illustration:** a trade closed at a loss; another trade has not yet occurred.  
**Possible self-report/model:** “the market went against me”; “I am experienced and cannot accept the error.”  
**Project interpretation:** restoring control/status may become an operational task, narrowing the field toward immediate re-entry or increased risk.  
**Supports:** P02–P06 and the idea that a participation point can exist after the loss but before the next trade.  
**Cannot support:** that all post-loss re-entry is emotional, irrational or identity-driven.

## D4. SP-EX-RETURN-001 — late detection after action

**Factual layer in the illustration:** automatic action has already occurred; additional continuation remains possible.  
**Project interpretation:** stopping escalation, reducing harm, repairing or changing a next-cycle condition can constitute real participation.  
**Supports:** P05/HCM-07; late detection does not equal total loss of participation.  
**Cannot support:** that a later alternative was available earlier, or that every harmful sequence can be safely repaired without external/professional support.

## D5. SP-EX-FOOD-001 / SP-EX-ALCOHOL-001 — immediate function vs delayed price

**Illustrative factual structure:** depleted/activated state → habitual means → immediate relief → delayed cost.  
**Project interpretation:** behavior can be locally functional relative to an urgent state-regulation task while conflicting with a broader direction.  
**Supports:** P01/P04/P07/P09/P10 and action-to-next-position logic.  
**Cannot support:** one universal cause for overeating or alcohol use, nor safe self-treatment of dependence.

## D6. SP-EX-TRAJECTORY-001 — action creates next position

**Illustrative chain:** overloaded day → depletion → alcohol → relief → poor sleep → more depletion.  
**Project interpretation:** realized continuations leave internal/external conditions that shape the next choice field.  
**Supports:** P10/HCM-05/HCM-08 and trajectory thinking.  
**Cannot support:** that the drawn alternative branch is equally accessible, safe or causal in all cases.

## D7. Status of real pilot evidence

**APPROVED boundary**  
The owner Health Lab is an active private evidence node, but one self-pilot does not establish Stage 1 efficacy or universal validity. Raw personal data remain private and are not imported into this public research packet as proof. Any future use must preserve source status, prompt exposure, selected-realized distinction and alternative explanations.

# E. Contradictions and tension map

## E1. Retrospective alternative vs actual availability

**Type:** `resolved_by_later_authority` / still instructional risk.

Early/easy wording can slide from “I can now imagine another continuation” to “I could have done that then.” P05, P12 and risk boundaries explicitly reject this inference.

**Safe reading:** retrospective reconstruction may discover a candidate participation point; historical availability remains a hypothesis unless supported by episode data.

## E2. Choice/insight language before RC-018 vs execution boundary

**Type:** `resolved_by_later_authority`.

Older Stage 1 material sometimes compresses “found/selected another move” and “changed the continuation.” RC-018 now requires:

```text
selection
≠ realization
```

**Safe reading:** Stage 1 capability requires at least some factual realized alteration, not merely an internally selected alternative.

## E3. “Consequence becomes feedback” language

**Type:** `resolved_by_later_authority`.

Some earlier formulations use “feedback” loosely for consequences. RC-018 distinguishes consequence, noticed data and used feedback.

**Safe reading:** consequences are potential information; feedback is only used when it changes or consciously confirms the next working model/continuation.

## E4. Operational task as useful concept vs post-hoc hidden-cause invention

**Type:** `possible_methodological_conflict`.

The concept is approved and central to P04/P08, but its content is usually inferred. Teaching it too confidently could make the model self-sealing: every action receives a plausible hidden function after the fact.

**Required Gate C protection:** operational task must remain a falsifiable/replaceable hypothesis; training should preserve competing explanations such as rational necessity, physiological need, skill deficit, external constraint or simple habit.

## E5. Linear episode chain vs reciprocal/multicausal reality

**Type:** `possible_methodological_conflict`.

The reconstruction sequence is pedagogically useful, but state, perception, body, environment and action can influence one another recursively. A clean sequence risks being mistaken for demonstrated causal order.

**Safe reading:** use the chain as an analytic representation of a concrete episode, not a claim of universal one-way causality.

## E6. P09 bodily “cause” language vs non-diagnostic boundary

**Type:** `wording_only` with empirical caution.

P09 legitimately includes body/state in the loop, but no universal bodily marker or direction of causality is approved.

**Safe reading:** body data are an input/output channel and possible leverage point, not a diagnostic truth source.

## E7. P10 trajectory framing vs personal authorship overreach

**Type:** `resolved_by_existing_boundary`.

The phrase “created next position” can be misread as “you created what happened to you.” P10 and SP-RISK-001/022 explicitly preserve independent external causes.

**Safe reading:** realized actions participate in trajectory; they do not monopolize causation.

## E8. One-episode Stage 1 vs repeated readiness criterion

**Type:** `wording_only` / important stage boundary.

Stage 1's unit of operation is one episode, while P12 requires repeated availability across several episodes. This is not Stage 2: repetition is used to establish that the **operation** is reusable, not to infer a cross-episode scenario.

## E9. Stage 1 reconstruction vs Stage 2 scenario recognition

**Type:** `resolved_by_stage_boundary`.

Stage 2 explicitly begins when several separate episode reconstructions can be held next to one another and cautiously compared. Stage 1 must not prematurely group them into “my scenario.”

## E10. AI/facilitator precision vs learner capability

**Type:** `possible_methodological_conflict`.

A sophisticated guide can create an excellent reconstruction that the learner merely recognizes. This would make the instrument, not the participant, capable.

**Required protection:** scaffold may hold structure; content, hypothesis testing, availability judgment and participation must belong substantially to the learner.

## E11. Better external outcome vs capability

**Type:** `resolved_by_existing_boundary`.

A good outcome may reflect easier conditions, external control, luck or absence of trigger. Conversely, a functional Stage 1 process may not produce the desired outcome.

**Safe reading:** outcome is data; capability evidence concerns the operation and realized participation.

## E12. Teaching simplicity vs epistemic precision

**Type:** `open`.

A simple chain is teachable; full epistemic labels may overload a beginner. Gate C/D must find the minimum distinctions that preserve safety without turning Stage 1 into an academic analysis task.

# F. Candidate psychological mechanisms — research hypotheses for Gate C

These are **HYPOTHESES**, not approved mechanisms. The objective is to propose the smallest plausible grouping that accounts for the canonical material while preserving competing explanations.

## F1. HYPOTHESIS M1 — Automatic assembly / epistemic fusion

**Working process**  
State, attention, perceived situation, activated self-image and local task become insufficiently distinguished, producing a felt “this is simply the situation / this is who I am / this is what must happen next.”

**Potential parameter coverage**  
P01, P02, P03, P06; part of P09.

**Source basis**  
Stage 1 state, reality-perception, self-perception and inevitability canon; HCM-01–HCM-04.

**Competing explanations**
- interpretation may be accurate;
- objective constraints may genuinely narrow choices;
- state-dependent cognitive narrowing may explain part of the effect without a single “fusion mechanism”;
- skill/resource deficit may be primary.

**Would weaken/falsify this grouping**  
If learners can reliably separate fact/model/self-image yet automatic continuation remains unchanged because the dominant issue is feasibility, skill or environmental constraint, M1 is insufficient as a core trainable unit.

**Trainability hypothesis**  
Likely trainable as a distinction operation, but may need decomposition if fact/model and self-image differentiation behave differently in practice.

---

## F2. HYPOTHESIS M2 — Local functional priority + habitual means

**Working process**  
A local operational task becomes dominant; a familiar action is highly available because it produces an immediate functional result (relief, control, role protection, avoidance, energy, etc.). The real need/function and the habitual means can collapse into one perceived necessity.

**Potential parameter coverage**  
P04, P06; major parts of P09; contributes to P07/P10 consequences.

**Source basis**  
Decision-driver theory, body contour, examples around food/alcohol/project/conflict.

**Competing explanations**
- rational short-term decision;
- genuine physiological need;
- simple habit without a meaningful hidden task;
- external demand or resource scarcity.

**Would weaken/falsify this grouping**  
If operational-task hypotheses do not improve prediction or intervention over simpler descriptions such as cue/habit/resource constraint, or if different “tasks” are largely post-hoc narratives.

**Trainability hypothesis**  
Potentially trainable through neutral functional inquiry (“what did the action do immediately?”) rather than requiring confident naming of hidden motive.

---

## F3. HYPOTHESIS M3 — Participation recovery through realistic availability

**Working process**  
The learner stops treating the whole episode as lost and identifies the first **actually available** continuation that can still change what happens next. Participation can re-enter before, during or after the first automatic move.

**Potential parameter coverage**  
P05, P08, P11; strengthened by RC-018.

**Source basis**  
Available-point canon, reconstruction canon, P11 functional profile, `SP-EX-RETURN-001`.

**Competing explanations**
- hindsight construction of options that were never available;
- facilitator/AI supplying the move;
- external circumstances, not improved participation, causing the change.

**Would weaken/falsify this grouping**  
If “available point” cannot be distinguished reliably from retrospective wishful thinking, or if changed continuations occur only when an external operator tells the participant exactly what to do.

**Trainability hypothesis**  
High relevance: this appears closest to the capability's practical core. Training must include the availability test and actual realization, not option brainstorming alone.

---

## F4. HYPOTHESIS M4 — Reality-coupled action → next-position feedback

**Working process**  
The learner connects habitual action to immediate result, delayed consequence and next factual position, then uses reality rather than explanation alone to update the next cycle.

**Potential parameter coverage**  
P07, P09, P10, P11; RC-018; HCM-05/HCM-06/HCM-08.

**Source basis**  
Trajectory/life manifestations; body delayed price; RC-018 Reality Loop.

**Competing explanations**
- consequences may be dominated by external factors;
- delayed/noisy outcomes make attribution uncertain;
- one action may have multiple or contradictory effects.

**Would weaken/falsify this grouping**  
If the learner can perform Stage 1 reconstruction/participation without needing trajectory framing, or if forcing causal attribution to “next position” creates more error than learning.

**Trainability hypothesis**  
Likely useful as a feedback discipline rather than as a single psychological mechanism: observe what actually happened, keep causal confidence explicit, update only what the data justify.

---

## F5. HYPOTHESIS M5 — Capability consolidation and anti-substitution

**Working process**  
The operation becomes repeatably available with limited structural support while insight, correct vocabulary, self-analysis and external guidance are prevented from substituting for the learner's own reconstruction and realized participation.

**Potential parameter coverage**  
P12, P13; also P08/P11; risks 024–027.

**Source basis**  
Transition criteria and stage trap.

**Competing explanation**  
This may not be a psychological mechanism of Stage 1 at all; it may be purely a training/assessment layer governing how core mechanisms are learned.

**Would weaken/falsify this grouping**  
If Gate C defines mechanisms strictly as processes that generate/change the automatic episode, M5 should be moved out of the mechanism map and retained as a cross-cutting learning-quality constraint.

**Trainability hypothesis**  
Essential as a training-system safeguard even if excluded from the final Psychological Mechanism Map.

# G. Open questions

## G1. Methodological — must be resolved before Gate C approval

1. **Is “automatic assembly / fusion” one mechanism or a shorthand for several processes?**  
   Gate C must avoid naming a single causal entity merely because P01–P06 form a coherent narrative.

2. **What status should “operational task” have in learning architecture?**  
   Recommended research position: useful functional hypothesis, never direct fact or universal hidden motive.

3. **Which parameters are mechanisms vs layers/constraints?**  
   Strong candidate distinction: P07 consequence/signal; P09 embodied layer; P10 trajectory scale; P11 practice requirement; P12 readiness; P13 anti-trap.

4. **Should reality-feedback be a mechanism cluster or a universal closure condition for every cluster?**  
   RC-018 suggests it may be better treated as a cross-cutting verification discipline.

5. **How much Stage 1 needs explicit trajectory framing?**  
   Enough to connect action to next position, but not so much that the learner is pushed into Stage 2/4 multi-episode analysis prematurely.

## G2. Psychological / empirical — may remain hypotheses through Gate C

- Does distinguishing fact/model/self-image causally increase option availability, or mainly improve retrospective description?
- Does identifying an operational task improve future choice beyond simple cue/behavior/consequence analysis?
- Which state/body variables most often reduce availability, and how context-specific are they?
- How reliably can people distinguish “psychologically unavailable” from “available but not realized”?
- What kinds of scaffolding improve learning without producing the content for the participant?
- What evidence shows transfer from retrospective reconstruction to earlier in-episode detection?

These questions do not need final answers to map candidate trainable processes, but Gate C must label them as unvalidated.

## G3. Instructional — must partly be resolved before Learning Units, not necessarily before Gate C

- What is the minimum beginner vocabulary that preserves fact/hypothesis boundaries?
- Should “operational task” be named explicitly or discovered through simpler functional questions?
- How to teach late participation without normalizing avoidable harm?
- How to prevent reconstruction from becoming a long self-analysis ritual?
- How to ensure examples remain illustrations rather than scripts into which users fit themselves?

## G4. Observability / measurement — may remain open until Gate F, but Gate C must preserve observability hooks

- What counts as evidence that a participation point was genuinely available?
- Which parts of reconstruction can be externally observed versus only self-reported?
- How should prompted vs unprompted capability be separated?
- What minimum record distinguishes selected from realized continuation?
- How should partial realization and conscious non-action be represented?
- How can repeated capability be evaluated without a premature composite score?

## G5. Boundary with Stage 2 — must be protected in Gate C

**APPROVED boundary**

```text
Stage 1 unit:
one concrete episode
→ distinguishable reconstruction
→ available participation
→ changed realized continuation

Stage 2 unit:
multiple separately reconstructed episodes
→ cautious functional comparison
→ candidate repeating scenario
```

Stage 1 may accumulate multiple episodes to show that the Stage 1 operation repeats. It must not require the learner to infer the repeating scenario; that is the next stage.

# H. Evidence boundaries

The following boundaries are mandatory for any Gate C/D/E work derived from this packet.

## H1. Internal approval ≠ external validation

`SP-S1-P01–P13` are internally approved Selection Point architecture and remain external-review/pilot-observability pending. A coherent system is not proof of universal psychological truth.

## H2. Coherent explanation ≠ proven causality

A sequence such as:

```text
state → perceived situation → self-image → operational task → action
```

is an analytic reconstruction. It does not by itself establish a causal chain, directionality or a single necessary mechanism.

## H3. Questionnaire completion ≠ SP capability

Measurement adherence is a separate operational variable. The instrument must not validate itself because the participant keeps using it.

## H4. Insight ≠ realized change

Understanding, vocabulary, reconstructed alternatives and internally selected continuation do not establish that factual trajectory changed.

## H5. Selected continuation ≠ realized continuation

RC-018 is mandatory. Internal choice remains important data, but only realized continuation receives external consequences as an executed event.

## H6. Desired outcome ≠ proof of Stage 1 capability

Good outcomes can arise from luck, easy conditions, external control or absence of trigger. Poor outcomes can occur despite a functional decision under uncertainty.

## H7. One successful episode ≠ mastery

P12 requires repeatability of the operation. Numeric thresholds remain unapproved and belong to later measurement work.

## H8. Prompted performance ≠ independent capability

Prompt exposure must remain visible. Structural support may help; it may not silently perform interpretation, availability judgment or action selection for the participant.

## H9. Self-report ≠ externally observed fact

Internal state, motive and intention often enter as self-report. Operational task and causal function are interpretations/hypotheses unless independently supported.

## H10. Repetition ≠ one hidden cause

Several similar outcomes or behaviors do not prove a shared scenario or motive. Stage 2 explicitly protects against premature pattern fusion.

## H11. Body data ≠ diagnosis

Bodily cues are data in a reciprocal system, not infallible signals of truth, choice or pathology.

## H12. Participation ≠ total control

Selection Point concerns available participation in further continuation, not authorship of every external event or guaranteed outcome.
