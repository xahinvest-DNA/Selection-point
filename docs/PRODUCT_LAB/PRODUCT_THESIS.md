## 1. Thesis

Selection Point is developed as a **bounded adaptive environment / simulator of selection and trajectory**.

The product is not defined by whether it is an app, course, game, chatbot or VR experience. Those are possible delivery forms. The product mechanism is the repeated experience of meaningful selection under changing conditions, followed by consequences, feedback and a separate check of transfer into reality.

The minimum useful loop is:

```text
representative situation
-> perceived position and constraints
-> Selection Point
-> available continuations
-> selected continuation
-> realized / non-realized continuation
-> consequences
-> feedback
-> changed next position
-> varied repetition
```

and, separately:

```text
simulation
-> real-world experiment
-> factual realization / non-realization
-> real consequence
-> review
-> next simulation
```

## 2. User problem

A person may:
- understand a desired direction;
- be able to explain what would be useful;
- sincerely select an intention in advance;
- still lose practical access to that choice in a meaningful real situation.

Knowledge alone therefore cannot be treated as evidence of Selection Capacity.

The product problem is not “the person lacks the correct advice”. It is:

> the desired distinction or continuation is not reliably available under the conditions in which it matters.

## 3. What the product trains

The environment should make it increasingly possible to:

- notice factual position and relevant constraints;
- recognize when an automatic continuation is taking over;
- distinguish fact from interpretation where that distinction changes action;
- preserve direction while changing route;
- distinguish selected continuation from realized continuation;
- notice consequences at more than one time scale;
- use feedback to update the next move;
- recover choice after disruption rather than require an ideal uninterrupted path;
- carry these distinctions from simulation into real situations.

## 4. What counts as training

Training is not primarily explanation.

A participant repeatedly meets structurally related situations whose surface conditions vary. The environment should make different continuations consequential and should expose what happens locally and over trajectory time.

If scaffolds or project language are used, they are secondary supports. They should fade as the distinction becomes practically available.

## 5. What counts as result

A meaningful result is not:
- finishing content;
- agreeing with the method;
- learning terminology;
- selecting the author-preferred answer in a simulator;
- reporting insight;
- being highly engaged.

Relevant evidence includes:
- earlier recognition of the same structure in changed conditions;
- less dependence on prompts;
- a practically available alternative continuation;
- clearer selected-versus-realized discrimination;
- preservation of direction through route change;
- a changed real action in a representative situation;
- use of consequences as feedback;
- repeatability and transfer.

## 6. Product boundary

Selection Point does not promise:
- universally correct decisions;
- total conscious control;
- elimination of automaticity;
- that simulated and real experience are equivalent;
- that simulator success guarantees real-world transfer;
- that a preferred behaviour is morally superior;
- that the system can diagnose the participant.

The product should preserve agency and reality-based falsifiability.

## 7. V0

V0 is deliberately small:
- one target capability;
- one domain: effective body / physical efficiency;
- 10–15 representative scenarios;
- repeated structural patterns under varied conditions;
- at least one multi-scale trajectory effect;
- one bounded real-world transfer experiment;
- manual/simple delivery allowed.

No VR, AI, platform, full curriculum or scoring economy is required.

## 8. Relationship to previous work

The earlier “trainer of the capacity to choose” thesis is retained and made more concrete: the trainer is now operationally defined as an **environment that generates repeated experience and tests transfer**.

Existing course and training artifacts are inputs, not the active product route:
- course lessons -> scenario seeds and language candidates;
- T0–T6 training architecture -> progression/prompt-fading research input;
- Reality Event Model -> real transfer instrumentation;
- trajectory work -> consequence/time-compression mechanics;
- Direction -> Route -> Action -> control distinction inside scenarios.

## 9. Falsification

The active product hypothesis is weakened if:

1. participants only learn which simulator answer is rewarded;
2. improvement disappears when prompts disappear;
3. participant understanding increases without a practical change;
4. real-world transfer cannot be observed;
5. the environment requires constant interpretation by an external leader;
6. consequences are arbitrary enough that no stable structure can be learned;
7. building the mechanism requires a large technical platform before a manual test is possible.

## 10. Current next question

What **single capability** should V0 make more practically available, and what **scenario grammar** can repeatedly expose it without turning the environment into a disguised quiz?

That question precedes interface design and implementation.
