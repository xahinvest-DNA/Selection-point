# Stage 1 Course Structure — Williams / Millman / Fritz Research

**ID:** SP-COURSE-S1-WMF-RES-001  
**Date:** 22 September 2026  
**Status:** research_complete / owner_interpretation_required  
**Reviewed structure:** SP-COURSE-S1-STRUCT-002

## Executive finding

The five-lesson structure remains defensible. The research does not justify adding another lesson.

The main missing element is a course-wide axis:

**chosen created direction + current reality**.

Without it, the course can drift into problem-solving: find an unwanted episode, explain it, stop it. Fritz's framework suggests a stronger orientation: define what is being created, see current reality accurately, then use episodes and actions in service of that created direction.

Two additional risks remain: the middle of the course can become too retrospective/analytical (Millman), and 'underlying structure' language can pull Stage 1 prematurely into Stage 2 pattern/scenario work (Williams/Fritz).

## Bill Williams

Trading Chaos is explicitly organized as graduated learning from novice to expert, with different goals and tools at different levels. The important principle is not the number five but level-appropriate maps and tools. Stage 1 therefore should remain narrow: one episode, simple models, direct experience, no Stage 2 pattern analysis and no full Foundation vocabulary dump.

Williams also foregrounds current reality, maps and underlying structure. This supports working from actual episodes and conditions instead of moral judgments. But Stage 1 should use only the local structure of one episode, not diagnose the participant's global underlying structure.

His framework also supports a conservative Stage 1 success criterion: gain a new perceptual/practical operation through experience, rather than demand body-goal success such as weight loss, zero alcohol or perfect compliance.

## Dan Millman

Millman's practical teaching repeatedly returns to three elements: daily life as the classroom, the present moment as the place of practice, and action rather than waiting for the preferred inner state.

This strongly supports the lesson -> real life -> debrief rhythm. Field practice is not homework added to the course; it is part of the course.

The main correction to the current structure is that Lessons 2–3 must not end in elegant retrospective explanation. Each should include a small present-action bridge when possible. Reflection is useful only if it returns the learner to current reality and action.

Using the body as the first training field is also coherent with Millman, but the body should not be reduced to weight, appearance or exercise performance. It is both a valued direction and a high-frequency feedback system.

## Robert Fritz

Fritz provides the strongest challenge to the current design. His central distinction is between problem solving (removing what is unwanted) and creative orientation (bringing into being a desired result). He treats current reality and desired result as the two data points that form structural tension.

For the body course this means that 'do not drink', 'do not overeat', 'do not skip training' and 'remove pain' cannot be the organizing goals. They may be useful local moves, but the course needs a positive created direction owned by the participant.

Recommended course-wide questions:

1. What do I want to create in relation to my body?
2. What is true now, without improvement or condemnation?

Fritz's primary/secondary choice distinction is also useful. The chosen body direction is primary; the next concrete action is a secondary choice in service of it. This strengthens Lesson 4: the available move should serve the created direction, not merely oppose the old behavior.

Fritz also reinforces the value of factual daily telemetry: current reality should not be polished into a positive story. The questionnaire can supply current-reality data while remaining separate from the course.

## Convergence

Across all three authors there is a strong shared core:

- reality before self-story;
- action matters more than verbal correctness;
- ordinary experience is where learning is tested;
- moral self-control is a weak organizing method;
- learning changes what information becomes visible and what action becomes naturally available.

Useful synthesis:

**Fritz gives direction. Millman gives immediacy and embodiment. Williams gives developmental discipline and level-appropriate maps. Selection Point supplies the episode-level mechanism and the Stage 1/Stage 2 boundary.**

## Productive tensions

Millman emphasizes now; Fritz emphasizes current reality plus a created future. These can be combined: future gives direction, the present is the only place action occurs. This directly matches the SP concern about local action without trajectory scale.

Williams/Fritz invite underlying-structure analysis; SP Stage 1 must stop earlier. At this stage: see enough local structure to participate, but do not diagnose the recurring system. Multiple-episode pattern comparison belongs to Stage 2.

Millman emphasizes action now, while SP accepts late noticing. Correct synthesis: if live action is available, act now; if not, repair now or change one condition of the next cycle. Late entry remains valid.

## Evaluation of SP-COURSE-S1-STRUCT-002

### Orientation

Keep it, but add two outputs: participant-owned created body result and factual current reality relative to it.

### Lesson 1 — One episode instead of 'I am like this'

Strongly supported. Refine episode selection: choose an episode because it matters to the created direction, not because it is the worst failure.

### Lesson 2 — How automatic continuation is assembled

Supported. Keep the chain minimal. Do not make mastery depend on naming every internal link or finding a hidden cause. End by clarifying what the episode reveals about current reality.

### Lesson 3 — State != familiar way of changing it

Strongly supported. Retain the question: what did the habitual action actually accomplish immediately? This makes the local path of least resistance visible without moralizing.

### Lesson 4 — Where participation remains possible

Needs the most important revision: find one available move **in service of the created direction under current reality**, not merely a move opposite to the old behavior.

### Lesson 5 — Did it -> reality answered

Very strong convergence. After consequences, update current reality and choose the next secondary move, if any. The created direction itself may also legitimately change after new information.

### Integration

Current Stage 2 boundary is correct. Preserve several episodes separately; do not yet search for the recurring scenario.

## Recommended revised spine

Orientation: chosen created direction + current reality.
Lesson 1: one concrete episode.
Lesson 2: local episode structure.
Lesson 3: state != familiar regulation means.
Lesson 4: one available move in service of the created direction.
Lesson 5: realization -> consequence -> updated current reality -> next move.
Integration: several separate comparable episodes.

Cross-cutting Millman layer: return to now -> body/contact with reality -> one physical action when available.

Cross-cutting Williams layer: only tools appropriate to the current learning level -> experience -> broader perception later.

## What should not be imported literally

From Williams: do not treat chaos-theory or brain-model claims as established general psychology.

From Millman: do not treat spiritual laws as scientific mechanisms or present-moment practice as a reason to ignore trajectory/history.

From Fritz: do not treat 'structure determines behavior' as a proven deterministic law or assume one fundamental choice automatically resolves behavior problems.

Use these as design hypotheses and practical maps. Reality remains the final arbiter.

## Recommended methodological decision

Revise SP-COURSE-S1-STRUCT-002 in four places only:

1. Orientation: add participant-owned created result + current reality.
2. Lesson 1: choose episodes relative to that direction.
3. Lesson 4: available move serves the created direction, not merely 'not the old move'.
4. Lesson 5: consequences update current reality and the next move.

Keep five core lessons, separate orientation, separate integration, Stage 2 boundary, and the daily questionnaire as an independent telemetry stream.
