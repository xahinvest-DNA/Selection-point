# Stage 1 Course — Work Brief

**ID:** SP-COURSE-S1-BRIEF-001  
**Status:** active  
**Date:** 20 September 2026  
**Mode:** course-first  
**Purpose:** build the learner-facing Stage 1 course directly.

## 1. Product objective

Create an educational module that can actually teach the first Selection Point stage to a real person.

The module should be understandable without reading Foundation documents or project governance.

It should contain enough theory to make the mechanism intelligible and enough practice to produce direct lived feedback.

## 2. Central course question

> **What must a person understand, notice and practice so that they begin to see the difference between an automatic continuation and an actually available next choice?**

This is a teaching question, not a measurement-system question.

## 3. Required learner outcome

By the end of Stage 1, the learner should have a grounded first-hand understanding that:

- the first reaction/impulse/story is not automatically the only continuation;
- facts, internal reactions and interpretations can be distinguished enough to change what happens next;
- understanding alone is not action;
- a chosen continuation must be realized to meet reality;
- missed or late noticing is data, not moral failure;
- ordinary life supplies practice situations.

The exact wording and sequence remain open to course design and testing.

## 4. Required course components

The Stage 1 course should include:

1. **Opening / hook** — why this matters in ordinary life.
2. **Core model** — simple explanation of automatic continuation and choice.
3. **Examples** — body, work, conflict, money/trading, avoidance and other ordinary domains.
4. **Primary distinctions** — only those needed by the learner.
5. **Practices** — simple, safe, repeatable exercises.
6. **Real-life assignment** — use the idea outside the lesson.
7. **Reflection** — extract feedback without turning it into self-judgment.
8. **Common traps** — intellectual understanding, over-analysis, fighting automaticity, “I failed,” retrospective storytelling.
9. **Integration** — what the learner carries into the next stage.
10. **Teacher notes** — what to clarify when the learner gets stuck.

## 5. Course-design criteria

The course is successful as a design if it is:

- simple enough to teach;
- deep enough not to become motivational advice;
- reality-coupled;
- non-moralizing;
- practical under ordinary load;
- consistent with Foundation boundaries;
- able to produce an experience the learner can recognize for themselves.

## 6. What is explicitly out of scope now

Do not make these prerequisites for course creation:

- full observability schema;
- validated measurement scale;
- post-protocol pilot architecture;
- participant scoring;
- questionnaire optimization;
- Gate closure artifacts;
- trainer/software implementation.

## 7. Research inputs

Use selectively:

### Foundation / canon
Primary source of approved meaning.

### Bill Williams
Development levels, perception/inner structure, feedback from reality, movement from novice toward expert functioning.

### Dan Millman
Present-moment contact, body, simple action, non-war with reality, practice in ordinary life.

### Gate-era Stage 1 research
Reference library only. Extract useful psychological distinctions, evidence and Red Team warnings.

### External scientific research
Use when it helps decide:
- what to teach;
- how to teach it;
- which exercise is plausible;
- where a claim needs qualification.

## 8. Immediate task

Before writing lessons, produce one compact **Stage 1 Teaching Architecture** answering:

1. Who is the learner at entry?
2. What is their central error/limitation?
3. What new distinction must become visible?
4. What experience should make that distinction real?
5. What sequence of 3–5 teaching steps gets them there?
6. What practices create the experience?
7. What mistakes will likely derail learning?
8. What shows, qualitatively, that the learner is ready to continue?

This Teaching Architecture is not another Gate. It is the outline of the course itself.

## 9. Working principle

> **Every theory element must earn its place by improving understanding or practice. Every practice must return the learner to reality.**
