## 1. Core principle

A lesson is not primarily a unit of information delivery.

A lesson is a **learning and reality-interaction cycle** in which the learner:

1. understands the one capacity being developed now;
2. receives the minimum map required for that operation;
3. sees the operation in a worked example;
4. performs it independently;
5. checks the result against facts rather than self-evaluation;
6. applies the capacity in ordinary life;
7. receives reality feedback;
8. enters the next lesson from an updated current position.

Working formula:

```text
created direction + current reality
→ one new capacity
→ minimum map
→ independent application
→ realized action / observation in ordinary life
→ consequence / reality response
→ updated current reality
```

The core lesson must function without a teacher or required AI assistant. Optional future software or support may assist the learner but may not be required for the lesson to work.

## 2. Course-wide axis

Every lesson remains inside two points.

### 2.1 Created direction

What the learner wants to create in relation to the body.

The organizing frame is not merely removal of a problem. Local moves such as not drinking, not overeating, not skipping training or reducing pain may be useful, but they are not automatically the primary created direction.

### 2.2 Current reality

What is factually true now, without improvement, condemnation or positive-story substitution.

Learning and action occur between these two points.

Therefore:

> **The skill is not the direction. The skill is a means of participating in movement from current reality toward a chosen created direction.**

The direction itself may later be revised when new reality justifies revision.

## 3. Universal lesson anatomy

### Block 1 — Current position

For the first lesson, establish:

- the participant-owned created direction;
- relevant factual current reality.

For later lessons, begin from the prior field cycle:

- what factually happened;
- what was actually realized;
- what consequences occurred;
- what is now true.

Do not organize this block around:

> “Did you succeed?”

Use instead:

> **“What factually happened?”**

The output is an updated current position, not a success score.

### Block 2 — One new capacity

Each lesson develops one new operation only.

The learner should know:

- what they are learning to do;
- why this capacity matters;
- how it serves the created direction;
- what observable result would show that the operation was actually performed.

Do not add extra operations for conceptual completeness.

### Block 3 — Minimum map

Provide only the model, distinction or vocabulary required for the current operation.

Rule:

> **Do not explain the whole system when one level-appropriate map is sufficient for the next operation.**

Stage 1 must not require:

- hidden-cause search;
- personality diagnosis;
- global underlying-structure diagnosis;
- repeating-scenario analysis;
- Stage 2 concepts or tasks;
- full Foundation vocabulary.

Stage 1 works with enough local structure to enable observation and participation in one episode.

### Block 4 — Worked example

Before independent application, show one clear completed example.

Structure:

```text
starting situation
→ application of the new operation
→ resulting output
```

The example demonstrates the operation, not merely the “correct answer.”

Prefer ordinary body-domain material such as:

- food;
- sleep;
- fatigue;
- training;
- recovery;
- physical workload;
- planning;
- ordinary daily choices.

The example need not be the learner's worst failure, most shameful behavior or most dramatic episode.

### Block 5 — Independent application

The learner applies the same operation to their own material.

The structure is held by the lesson itself through:

- a bounded sequence of steps;
- a work form or card;
- a limited number of fields;
- explicit boundaries for the current operation.

The learner generates the content. The material holds the methodological boundary.

The practical form is not a daily questionnaire. It exists to help perform one learning operation.

### Block 6 — Reality check

Replace self-evaluation such as:

> “Did I do this correctly?”

with factual discrimination such as:

- Am I working with one concrete episode rather than my whole life or identity?
- What here is directly observable fact?
- What is my interpretation?
- What do I actually know?
- What do I not know?
- Have I crossed beyond the operation of this lesson?
- Does the output correspond to factual reality?

`Unknown / I do not know` is a valid result.

The check evaluates the quality of discrimination, not the worth of the learner.

### Block 7 — Field practice

Field practice is not homework appended after the lesson. It is **the second part of the lesson itself**.

The instructional environment provides a map and first application. Ordinary life provides the real test.

Give one bounded field task appropriate to the lesson, for example to:

- notice;
- distinguish;
- realize;
- stop or reduce an ongoing continuation;
- repair a consequence;
- change one condition;
- prepare support;
- verify a fact.

Do not require:

- constant all-day self-monitoring;
- global behavior control;
- waiting for a dramatic lapse;
- deliberate creation of a difficult or unsafe situation.

Ordinary naturally occurring life is sufficient.

### Block 8 — Realization

Keep separate:

```text
understood
≠ selected
≠ realized
```

In field work, capture what actually occurred, not merely what the learner intended.

If a live action was available, distinguish whether it was actually performed.

If live action was not available, valid Stage 1 continuations may include:

- late noticing and reconstruction;
- stopping or reducing a remaining continuation;
- repairing a consequence;
- changing one condition of the next cycle;
- preparing a resource or support;
- seeking appropriate help.

Late noticing is not lesson failure.

### Block 9 — Reality response

After field application, return to consequences rather than asking whether “the method worked.”

Questions may include:

- What did I actually do?
- What happened after that?
- What changed?
- What did not change?
- What did I expect that did not occur?
- What is factually true now?

A desired outcome is not required for a valid learning cycle.

The essential event is that a realized continuation meets reality and produces information.

### Block 10 — Observable learning trace

Each lesson leaves an observable trace of learning.

It need not always be a document. It may be:

- one bounded episode record;
- a completed local chain or distinction;
- facts separated from interpretations;
- a selected and realized action;
- an environmental change;
- an observed consequence;
- an updated factual current position.

Rule:

> **Forms serve learning. Learning does not serve form completion.**

The daily trajectory questionnaire, if later redesigned and re-authorized, remains a separate telemetry instrument rather than the lesson artifact.

## 4. Connection between lessons

The five lessons are not independent chapters.

Each later lesson uses the result of the previous cycle.

```text
Lesson N
→ field practice
→ realized action / observation
→ consequence
→ updated current reality
→ Lesson N+1
```

The next lesson begins from the position produced by the prior cycle, not merely from a new topic.

## 5. Binding methodological rules

### 5.1 Fact is not interpretation

Both may exist, but they must not be silently collapsed into one another.

### 5.2 Unknown is valid

The learner does not invent a cause merely to complete a form.

### 5.3 Late noticing is valid

Participation does not exist only before the first automatic reaction.

### 5.4 Direction is not route is not action

```text
direction ≠ route ≠ next step ≠ realized action
```

An ineffective route or action does not automatically invalidate the direction.

### 5.5 Inner selection is not realization

A decision or intention is recorded separately from what was actually done.

### 5.6 Reality has priority over explanation

A compelling theory, correct answer or strong intention does not replace observable consequence.

### 5.7 Do not center the weakest link

Choose episodes because they are relevant to the created direction, not because they represent the learner's worst behavior.

No single weakness, including alcohol or another salient behavior, becomes the default center of attention.

### 5.8 One operation per lesson

Do not leak later-level operations into the current lesson for explanatory completeness.

### 5.9 Safety has priority

No exercise requires unsafe action, interruption of necessary skilled activity, avoidance of appropriate medical or other professional help, or disregard of physical limits.

## 6. Cross-cutting design layers

### 6.1 Fritz layer — created direction + current reality

The primary axis is:

```text
what do I want to create?
+
what is true now?
→
what continuation serves that direction under current reality?
```

Skills, routes and concrete moves are means rather than substitutes for the created result.

### 6.2 Millman layer — ordinary life + now + action

Reflection must return to present reality and physical realization when genuinely available.

Field practice is part of the lesson itself rather than optional homework.

### 6.3 Williams layer — level-appropriate maps and tools

Use only the concepts needed at the current developmental level.

Experience should expand perception before broader maps are introduced.

Do not import Williams's chaos/brain claims as established general psychology, and do not use “underlying structure” as permission for Stage 1 global diagnosis.

## 7. What is retained, transformed or removed from v2

| v2 element | v3 treatment |
|---|---|
| Worked example | Retained |
| One explicit model/distinction | Retained and tightened to minimum map |
| Field assignment | Retained and promoted to second half of the lesson |
| Capture | Strengthened into observable learning trace |
| Teacher-led debrief | Replaced by factual current-position reconstruction |
| Guided self-application | Replaced by bounded independent application |
| Teacher correction | Replaced by reality-check criteria |
| Adaptive dialogue | May become explicit prewritten `if → then` branches only where necessary |
| Teacher/AI safety control | Replaced by embedded safety boundaries |
| Required teacher/AI | Removed |
| Daily questionnaire as lesson mechanism | Excluded; telemetry remains separate |

## 8. Course rhythm

The course remains event-informed rather than rigidly calendar-gated.

The approved sequence remains:

```text
orientation
→ lesson 1
→ field practice / reality response
→ lesson 2
→ field practice / reality response
→ lesson 3
→ field practice / reality response
→ lesson 4
→ field practice / reality response
→ lesson 5
→ repeated real cycles
→ integration
```

Five core lessons remain approved. Orientation and integration are not additional lessons.

The exact pacing remains a design hypothesis to be tested later; this template does not authorize an external pilot.

## 9. Quality criterion for the lesson architecture

A lesson is not complete merely because the learner understood the material or filled in a form.

The intended learning transition is:

```text
before:
the learner cannot yet perform the target operation with sufficient distinction

→ receives a level-appropriate map and example
→ performs the operation independently
→ uses it in ordinary life
→ receives reality feedback

then:
the learner enters the next relevant situation with a more developed capacity to participate in its continuation
```

This is the controlling architecture for Stage 1 lesson redesign.
