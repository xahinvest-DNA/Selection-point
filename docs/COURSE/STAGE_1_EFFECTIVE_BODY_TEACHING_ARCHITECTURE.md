
# Stage 1 — “Эффективное тело” Teaching Architecture

**ID:** SP-COURSE-S1-TA-001  
**Status:** working_draft_for_owner_review  
**Date:** 20 September 2026  
**Course mode:** course-first  
**Foundation stage:** Stage 1 — «Жизнь внутри автоматизма»  
**Field of practice:** body / health / physical efficiency  
**Purpose:** define the teaching spine before writing individual lessons.

## 1. Course direction

The project previously used several compatible formulations.

### Product-facing direction

> **Эффективное тело.**

### Approved operational formulation

> **Системно выбирать и фактически совершать действия, которые сохраняют здоровье, повышают физическую эффективность и расширяют возможности тела в будущем.**

### Short human formulation

> **Тело со временем становится здоровее, сильнее и способнее.**

### Daily trajectory question

> **То, что я сделал сегодня, увеличило или уменьшило возможности моего тела завтра?**

### Navigation metaphor

> **Вес — показатель. Похудение — возможный манёвр. Здоровье — направление. Способность управлять своей траекторией здоровья — навык.**

These formulations describe different levels of the same goal and should not be collapsed into “lose weight.”

## 2. What the Stage 1 course is for

The first stage does not teach a complete healthy lifestyle and does not ask the learner to solve all habits.

It teaches the first transferable operation:

> **Take one concrete body/life episode that previously looked like “it just happened” and begin to see it as a sequence in which state, perception, habitual continuation, immediate result and next position can be distinguished — and where at least one realistic participation point can eventually be found and acted on.**

Foundation formulation preserved:

~~~text
automatic result
→ concrete episode
→ distinguishable links
→ available participation
→ changed continuation
~~~

The learner does not need to prevent the first reaction perfectly.

## 3. Learner at entry

Typical entry condition:

- the person usually knows many “correct” health recommendations;
- knowledge does not reliably become behavior;
- the person sees results more clearly than the chain that produced them;
- body state can feel like an instruction:
  - tired → “I need alcohol/food/phone/rest in this exact form”;
  - hungry → “I need this available food now”;
  - stressed → “I need immediate relief”;
- real bodily need can merge with one familiar method of meeting it;
- the person often explains recurrence through willpower, character, circumstances or “I am just like this”;
- after consequences, the opposite automatic response may appear: harsh control, guilt, new radical promise.

The Stage 1 learner is not stupid or uninformed.

The problem is that the automatic process is insufficiently differentiated while it is happening.

## 4. Central Stage 1 teaching error

The central error is not:

> “I have an automatic reaction.”

Automaticity is normal.

The central error is:

~~~text
state / impulse / interpretation / familiar means
are experienced as one obvious reality
→
the continuation feels inevitable
~~~

Body-specific form:

~~~text
real need
=
habitual means of changing the state
~~~

Examples:

~~~text
need recovery
=
need alcohol

need energy
=
need this food

need reduce tension
=
need phone / avoidance

feel exhausted
=
participation is impossible
~~~

## 5. The new distinction that must become visible

Core distinction:

> **Состояние реально. Импульс реален. Но ни состояние, ни импульс сами по себе не являются приказом на конкретное продолжение.**

Supporting distinctions:

~~~text
body sensation
≠ its meaning
≠ the real need
≠ the familiar response

fact
≠ interpretation

impulse
≠ mandatory action

immediate relief
≠ full consequence

understanding
≠ participation

selection
≠ realization
~~~

The learner does not need to memorize these as terminology.

The course must make them experientially visible.

## 6. The experience the learner should have

The critical learning experience is not “I controlled myself.”

It is closer to:

> **“I thought I simply wanted / needed / could not do otherwise. When I looked at one concrete episode, I saw a sequence. The usual action actually solved something immediately, but it also created the next position. And I found at least one place where I could participate differently — even if I noticed it late.”**

This experience must come from a real episode, not only from explanation.

## 7. Proposed teaching sequence

### Step 1 — The body tells the truth about repetition, not about moral worth

Goal:
- remove the “I already know what to do” illusion;
- show the difference between knowledge and repeated realization.

Core idea:

> **Тело реагирует не на качество объяснения, а на то, что человек реально повторяет.**

Teaching examples:
- sleep;
- portions;
- alcohol;
- recovery;
- movement;
- training.

Practice:
- choose one recent concrete body episode with a visible consequence.

No analysis yet.

### Step 2 — Turn “it happened” into one concrete episode

Goal:
- stop global self-description;
- reconstruct only one event.

Learner-facing chain:

~~~text
what was happening
→ what state I was in
→ what the situation seemed to mean
→ what I wanted to change immediately
→ what I actually did
→ what it gave me right away
→ what position it created afterward
~~~

Important:
- hidden psychological cause is not required;
- “I do not know” remains valid;
- immediate benefit of the old route is acknowledged rather than denied.

Practice:
- reconstruct one real episode.

### Step 3 — Separate the need from the familiar means

Goal:
- create the first practical opening.

Examples:

~~~text
I need recovery
≠ I necessarily need alcohol

I need energy
≠ I necessarily need sugar / overeating

I need reduce load
≠ I must abandon the whole day

I need emotional relief
≠ I must continue the old route
~~~

Practice:
- in the reconstructed episode identify:
  1. what state/need was real;
  2. what familiar means was used;
  3. what the means gave immediately;
  4. what it created next.

This is not a command to choose another means yet. First the fusion must become visible.

### Step 4 — Find the first realistically available participation

Goal:
- destroy the standard of perfect early control.

Question:

> **Какой участок стал различим первым и что после него ещё реально можно было изменить?**

Possible participation:
- stop after beginning;
- reduce scale;
- not add the next continuation;
- repair a consequence;
- return after a miss;
- ask for help;
- change one condition of the next recurrence;
- make one prepared real-world move.

Practice:
- perform one actual continuation that is still available now or in the next recurrence.

The course does not count a retrospective fantasy as a real available move.

### Step 5 — Reality closes the lesson

Goal:
- prevent “I understood it” from becoming completion.

Cycle:

~~~text
reconstruction
→ one real move
→ consequence
→ next position
→ what changed for the next episode?
~~~

Practice:
- return with what actually happened, including non-realization.

No shame score.

## 8. Candidate practices

These are course-design candidates, not yet final lesson scripts.

### Practice A — One episode, not my whole life

Take one specific event from the last 24–48 hours and describe only observable sequence.

### Practice B — Need ≠ familiar means

Complete:

> “В том эпизоде мне действительно было нужно ________. Привычным способом получить это было ________.”

Then test whether those are actually the same thing.

### Practice C — Immediate result / next position

Two columns:

~~~text
what the action gave immediately
what it created for the next position
~~~

### Practice D — Late entry

If the first reaction already happened:

> **Что ещё не завершено?**

Then make one real continuation where safe.

This is not a special directed-question intervention; it is a course exercise derived from the Stage 1 Foundation principle of available participation.

### Practice E — Reality return

After one actual move:

> **Что реально произошло?**

No need to prove that the method “worked.”

## 9. Common learning traps

### Trap 1 — Understanding replaces action

> “Теперь я понял.”

Course response:
- one real continuation is required before closing the episode.

### Trap 2 — The learner searches for a hidden true cause

> “This must be my trauma / fear / ego / sabotage.”

Course response:
- return to the concrete episode;
- cause may remain unknown.

### Trap 3 — Fighting automaticity

> “I must eliminate these reactions.”

Course response:
- automaticity is not the enemy;
- the training target is distinguishability and participation.

### Trap 4 — Perfect early noticing

> “If I noticed only afterward, I failed.”

Course response:
- late entry is valid;
- next continuation may still be open.

### Trap 5 — Retrospective omnipotence

> “I can see another option now, so I definitely could have done it then.”

Course response:
- imagined option ≠ live available option.

### Trap 6 — Global identity

> “I am weak / lazy / addicted / undisciplined.”

Course response:
- return to one episode and its facts.

### Trap 7 — The teacher explains the learner

Course response:
- teacher may hold structure;
- learner must produce the content and participation.

## 10. Relationship to the daily trajectory questionnaire

The questionnaire and course have different jobs.

~~~text
COURSE
teaches a new operation

QUESTIONNAIRE
records the trajectory over time
~~~

The questionnaire should not contain the entire Stage 1 lesson every evening.

The course can use questionnaire history as raw material for examples, but questionnaire completion is never course mastery.

## 11. Qualitative readiness to continue

The learner is approaching the next stage when, across more than one concrete episode, they can increasingly:

- stay with one episode rather than global identity;
- reconstruct a functionally connected chain without inventing certainty;
- separate facts from interpretations/hypotheses enough to work;
- recognize that immediate relief and later position are different;
- identify a realistically available participation point;
- actually change or prepare at least some continuation;
- use consequences as information;
- hold several episodes separately without prematurely declaring one global scenario.

No fixed number of days, episodes or success percentage is set here.

## 12. What the course should not become

Not:
- a diet;
- a weight-loss challenge;
- alcohol treatment;
- a fitness plan;
- a willpower program;
- a self-monitoring marathon;
- a hidden-cause therapy;
- a motivational lecture.

It is a course in the first Selection Point operation, taught through the concrete field of the body.
