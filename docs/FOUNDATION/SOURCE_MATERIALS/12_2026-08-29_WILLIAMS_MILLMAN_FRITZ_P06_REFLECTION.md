## Boundary

This file reconstructs how three frameworks might answer the three reflection questions for SP-S4-P06. It does **not** claim that Bill Williams, Dan Millman or Robert Fritz formulated Selection Point, and it does not replace the owner's reflection or the later Architect pass.

Primary / near-primary anchors used:

- Dan Millman: official Peaceful Warrior materials on moment-to-moment practice, presence, behavior, acceptance of emotions, control vs influence and surrender.
- Robert Fritz: official writings on structural tension, vision + current reality, creative orientation, primary/secondary choices and underlying structure.

## Question 1

**When a meaningful direction has already been chosen, but under load one particular move begins to feel almost mandatory, what false conclusion becomes most convincing?**

### Bill Williams — reconstruction

The likely mistake is to confuse a trader's own current belief/reading with the market itself.

Working false equality:

```text
I feel in sync / I recognize the structure
=
this interpretation is the market
=
this concrete move is what must happen now
```

Williams repeatedly separates the market from the trader's belief system and treats alignment with the market as an ongoing relationship rather than permission to make one's reading infallible. The more experienced the trader becomes, the more dangerous it may be to turn educated intuition into certainty.

Possible P06 signal from Williams:

> **Mastery can create the illusion that a strongly experienced reading is no longer a reading.**

### Dan Millman — reconstruction

The likely mistake is to confuse presence with a particular internal feeling: clarity, calm, certainty or lack of conflict.

Working false equality:

```text
I am truly present / centered
=
I should feel clear and undivided
=
the right action should be obvious
```

Millman's official materials repeatedly return to moment-to-moment attention, action without first fixing thoughts/emotions, acceptance of emotions and surrender to change. Therefore tension, doubt or fear do not themselves prove that presence or choice has disappeared.

Possible P06 signal from Millman:

> **The practice can be mistaken for the state that sometimes accompanies the practice.**

### Robert Fritz — reconstruction

The likely mistake is to let the chosen outcome or direction dictate the next move without a fresh reading of current reality.

Working false equality:

```text
primary choice is clear
=
secondary choice is already determined
```

Fritz's structural tension contains two irreducible poles: vision and current reality. The chosen outcome organizes secondary choices, but current reality remains co-determining and learning may be required. Direction therefore does not logically prescribe one move.

Possible P06 signal from Fritz:

> **A clear direction can be mistaken for a complete instruction.**

## Question 2

**If a Stage-4 person can usually preserve choice under load but one day collapses into an automatic move, what false interpretation of the lapse is most likely?**

### Bill Williams — reconstruction

Likely false conclusion:

> **I lost mastery / I am no longer in sync / my system failed.**

A Williams-style correction would treat the episode as information about the trader's still-active underlying structure and belief system. A lapse can show where an old structure regained the path of least resistance; it does not by itself erase the previously developed capacity.

### Dan Millman — reconstruction

Likely false conclusion:

> **If I were really a peaceful warrior, this would not have happened.**

Millman explicitly describes life as a series of moments and says that some moments show clarity and compassion while others do not. Practice therefore cannot honestly be converted into a permanent identity or a guarantee of uninterrupted centeredness.

### Robert Fritz — reconstruction

Likely false conclusion:

> **The reversal proves that my vision was false or that I am back at zero.**

A structural reading is different: the lapse is part of current reality. It may reveal that an older underlying structure still has force in this context. That is information for redesign and the next creative cycle, not a global verdict on the person or the chosen result.

### Shared reconstruction

```text
local loss of access
→ falsely becomes

global verdict about self / path / method
```

This may be an important Stage-4 candidate because a person with real prior success now has something new to lose: the identity of being someone who "can preserve choice under load."

## Question 3

**What dangerous promised reward can Stage 4 itself create: “If I really master this, then I will never again… / will always be able to… / will no longer need to…”?**

### Bill Williams — reconstruction

Dangerous extrapolation:

> **If I become sufficiently aligned with the market and my own structure, I will no longer be meaningfully uncertain; the right move will become naturally obvious.**

Williams' language about higher trader levels, flow and alignment makes this misreading especially tempting. But the market remains nonlinear/chaotic, and a trader still trades through a belief system. Alignment cannot honestly be upgraded into omniscience or guaranteed control.

### Dan Millman — reconstruction

Dangerous extrapolation:

> **If I master the practice, I will remain centered, peaceful and present regardless of what happens.**

Millman's own descriptions resist this: waves of change cannot be fully predicted or controlled, emotions do not first need to be fixed, and even mature practice remains moment-to-moment.

### Robert Fritz — reconstruction

Dangerous extrapolation:

> **If I install the right underlying structure, I will no longer need to re-enter the creative process; the structure will carry me automatically to the result.**

Fritz does argue that structure strongly shapes behavior. The overreach begins when this becomes a guarantee of outcome or a reason to stop updating current reality. His own creative process repeatedly requires both vision and current reality, with learning and secondary choices along the way.

## Comparative synthesis — not canon

Three different failure modes converge around one possible Stage-4 illusion:

```text
real developed capacity
→ successful experience preserving choice under load
→ capacity is reinterpreted as a stable guarantee
→ "if I really mastered this, load should no longer be able to reorganize me"
```

Possible formulations to test later:

1. **Formed choice = immunity to load.**
2. **Mastery = permanent access to the right response.**
3. **If the direction is truly mine and the skill is real, I should not lose clarity.**
4. **The ability to preserve choice is mistaken for control over the state in which choice must be made.**

The fourth formulation appears the most architecturally distinct from S3-P06, but it is not yet approved and may still collapse into P01/P05 under Red Team.

Potential vertical:

```text
S1:
current assembly = necessity

S2:
moving away from old = moving toward my own

S3:
chosen task = already formed organizer

S4 candidate:
formed ability to choose = immunity from load / control over access to choice
```

## Key falsification question for later Architect pass

Does this candidate add a new practically useful distinction beyond:

- P01: access to trained ability varies with load;

- P03: self-model can acquire practical weight;

- P05: available move field can collapse?

If not, SP-S4-P06 should receive `zero-delta` or be reformulated.
