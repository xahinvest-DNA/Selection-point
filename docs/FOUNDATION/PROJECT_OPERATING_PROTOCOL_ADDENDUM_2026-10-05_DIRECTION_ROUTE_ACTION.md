# Addendum — Direction → Route → Action

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Status:** owner-approved methodological working proposition; must remain active in near-term Selection Point work  
**Scope:** cross-cutting architecture, Stage 1 / Effective Body, training and product interpretation  

## 1. Owner observation

Andrey formulated the following observation during the Health Lab:

> «Какое направление я бы ни выбрал, усилия для движения в этом направлении придётся прилагать. Если я хочу попасть из точки А в точку Б, то мне необходимо прикладывать к этому силы, время, внимание, способности. Нравится ли мне это — вопрос другой. С другой стороны, если мне не нравится ехать из точки А в точку Б на автобусе, я могу прогуляться, взять велосипед, поехать на машине — варианты на любой вкус и цвет есть всегда. Первое, что необходимо — это проложить маршрут. И не обязательно сразу идеальный».

This observation is retained as an active methodological input and must not be reduced to a Health Lab note.

## 2. Required distinctions

Selection Point must distinguish at least three levels:

1. **Direction** — where the person intends to move / what trajectory is being built.
2. **Route** — the current way of moving in that direction.
3. **Next step / realized action** — the concrete action actually performed from the current position.

Therefore:

```text
direction ≠ route ≠ next step ≠ realized action
```

A person may preserve the direction while changing the route. Failure, cost or dislike of a particular route does not by itself falsify the direction.

## 3. Effort is not an anomaly

Choosing a direction does not remove the cost of movement. Movement may require time, attention, physical or cognitive effort, capabilities and opportunity cost.

The method must not implicitly promise that the correct direction or correct choice will make effort disappear.

At the same time, effort is not a reason to freeze one route. If one mode is unsuitable, another route may be tested while preserving the direction.

## 4. Route does not need to be ideal before movement begins

The system must not require a complete optimal route before action.

Working cycle:

```text
direction
→ provisional route
→ next step
→ realized action
→ consequences / feedback
→ new factual position
→ route correction
→ next step
```

The route is progressively specified through contact with reality.

Short working formula:

> **Direction can remain stable while the route changes on the basis of feedback.**

## 5. Relation to RC-018

This addendum does not replace the approved `choice → realized continuation → feedback` boundary.

It adds a higher-order context around that cycle:

```text
direction / trajectory intent
→ current route
→ factual position
→ available Selection Point
→ selected continuation
→ realized continuation
→ consequences
→ interpreted feedback
→ correction of route and/or next step
→ next factual position
```

`selected ≠ realized` remains mandatory. A route exists operationally only through realized actions and their consequences, not through internal choice alone.

## 6. Relation to scale

Local dissatisfaction with a step or route must not automatically be interpreted as evidence that the direction is wrong.

Conversely, a locally acceptable action must not automatically be treated as evidence that the longer trajectory is correct.

Direction, route and action therefore need evaluation at different time scales.

## 7. Stage 1 / Effective Body implication

The Stage 1 direction remains the Effective Body objective. Specific practices are means, not the objective itself.

Training, sleep, food, recovery, workload reduction, an alternative to alcohol, or another action may each be the appropriate next step depending on the factual position.

Therefore avoid absolute rules such as:

```text
training = always the correct action
alternative-to-alcohol = the goal
maximum effort = progress
```

Instead ask whether the realized step is consistent with the direction given the current position and available feedback.

## 8. Health Lab observation that triggered the clarification

On 2026-10-05 the participant had 5 hours of sleep and high overall load. A fleeting alcohol impulse appeared, but alcohol was not used. The participant deliberately chose not to follow the first impulse and completed 20 minutes of kettlebell training despite strong fatigue.

This episode is evidence for the research record, not proof of a universal rule. Its methodological value is that movement in a chosen direction occurred **without requiring the undesired impulse to disappear first**.

The same episode must not be generalized into `train regardless of condition`; feedback may make rest, sleep, food or load reduction the better next step on another day.

## 10. Compact statement

```text
Choose a direction.
Build a workable first route, not a perfect one.
Make the next real move.
Use feedback from reality.
Correct the route.
Continue from the new factual position.
```
