## 2. Внешняя проверка новой гипотезы

### Facial feedback

- Coles, Larsen & Lench (2019), PMID 30973236: meta-analysis 138 studies / 286 effects — facial feedback effect significant but small and heterogeneous.
- Many Smiles Collaboration (2022), PMID 36266452, DOI 10.1038/s41562-022-01458-9: n=3878, 19 countries; voluntary facial action and mimicry could amplify/initiate happiness; pen-in-mouth evidence less conclusive.

### Posture

- Nair et al. (2015), PMID 25222091: randomized posture manipulation altered several affective / stress-response measures.
- Wilkes et al. (2017), PMID 27494342: brief upright-posture manipulation changed positive affect/fatigue in a mild-to-moderate depressive-symptom sample.
- Lin et al. (2026), PMID 41619371: posture effects on mood recovery were moderated by embodied experience; not universal.

### Respiration

- Homma & Phillips (2022), PMID 35965025;
- Heck et al. (2022), PMID 35292337;
- review 2025, PMID 40204908.

These sources support reciprocal breathing ↔ emotion/cognition and respiration-linked modulation of distributed neural activity, perception and action.

### Mental imagery

- Holmes & Mathews (2010), PMID 20116915;
- Pictet et al. (2011), PMID 22032936.

Mental imagery can causally evoke or modify affect; it is not itself a bodily manipulation but supports the broader dynamic-system framing of state.

## 3. What the counterexample successfully falsified

Falsified / corrected:

```text
body = only readout / indicator of internal state
```

More accurate:

```text
body
= output of state
+
input into subsequent state dynamics
+
part of the current position
```

But this causal role is probabilistic and context-dependent.

## 4. What the counterexample did NOT establish

It did not establish:

1. a unique bodily pattern of S4;
2. a universal bodily switch of choice;
3. that smiling, posture, breathing or imagery reliably restore choice for every person/context;
4. that improving subjective mood equals restoring functional participation;
5. that bidirectional body-state causality begins only at Stage 4.

## 5. Stage-specificity attack

The revised candidate is:

> **body-state relations are bidirectional, and changing bodily organization can sometimes alter emotional/cognitive state.**

Red Team question:

> Is this specifically SP-S4-P09?

Result: **No.**

The mechanism is cross-stage and partly already contained in the foundation:

- HCM-02: current state changes perception and available actions;
- HCM-02: some actions themselves change state;
- S1-P09: the body is an initial condition, carrier of reaction and result of prior action;
- S3-P09: bodily state affects the available field without fully determining action.

Thus the new information reveals a **cross-cutting clarification**, not a unique S4 bodily manifestation.

## 6. Category-boundary attack: P09 vs P11

If we formulate:

> deliberately change smile / posture / breathing / movement to modify current availability,

we are no longer describing a bodily manifestation. We are describing a functional class of intervention/practice.

That belongs primarily to SP-S4-P11 / later Phase 5.

Therefore P09 should not be rescued by importing practice content.

## 7. Revised zero-delta recommendation

Previous weak reason:

> there is no unique bodily marker of S4.

Revised stronger reason:

> **The body is an active causal participant in the state–perception–action loop, not merely a marker. However, this bidirectionality is a cross-stage property of the human system and does not create a new stage-specific bodily architecture for S4. What becomes especially useful at S4 is the possibility of using an available bodily component as one route to modify state/availability, but that is a P11 functional-practice question, not P09 “manifestations”.**

Therefore current recommendation remains:

> **SP-S4-P09 — `zero-delta`, with amended cross-cutting boundary.**
